# Configuration

Sea Haven has no runtime configuration surface (no accounts, no server); everything below is a URL parameter, build-time, or repo plumbing.

## URL parameters

The running game reads its whole situation off the URL, which is what makes a level a link and a bug report a repro:

| Parameter  | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `seed`     | The level seed. Every shore, sea, gate and ramp is generated from it; the same seed is the same level on every machine — on the coast `biome` names. Naming one also takes the PINNED shore off (see `mode`), so a link that asks for a seed rides that seed in every mode.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `biome`    | The start card's COAST row (FREE's card; the other three modes take the coast from the pinned shore they are on): which biome the seed is built on — `taiga` (a cold sheltered skerry coast: granite, pine, grey-green brackish water, the sea broken up by the islands), `mangrove` (a warm flat salt coast: white sand, mangrove and palms, clear turquoise water with a long groundswell coming in over the sandbars), `arctic` (a polar coast: a wall of glacier ice over black water at the freezing point, bergs off it, and in its winter a sheet of ice with a channel cut down the course) or `karst` (a warm-temperate limestone coast: bare white rock falling steeply into the bluest, clearest water in the game, pebble coves, pine and maquis, islets and reefs everywhere, a short steep sea off the land). The same seed is a different shore on each. A setting like the rows beside it, so a link that names one is the run START rides.                                                                                                                                                        |
| `track`    | `circuit` deals the seed as an OCEAN CIRCUIT (R29–R31): a closed lap ridden two or three times round that starts at the beach, tracks the shore, runs out into the open sea to round its lit buoys and comes back in — with no river anywhere. Anything else (or nothing) is the default coast sprint. The HUD grows a LAP chip on a circuit and loses it on a sprint.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `mode`     | The front door's tile after CAMPAIGN (the campaign is a page, `menu=campaign`, not a mode: each of its levels is a race or a tricks run pinned to one shore and one day): `race` (eleven others on the grid, everybody off on one GO, hulls that can be leaned on, the tricks off), `tricks` (the shore with the course taken off it — no gates, the ramps left standing, a clock that runs down and a score that runs up), `timeTrial` (the course against the clock, alone) or `free` (the same water with nothing asked of it: the same three lights in front of it, but no buzzer and nobody else out there). The first three ride one of the CAMPAIGN's pinned shores, picked on the level card (`menu=levels`) and carrying its own day, so two times down one of them are two times down the same water; `free` is the one that picks a SEED, sets the day by hand — its WIND and WAVES rows are faders rather than ladders, and its wind always blows dead onshore — and rides a speed class other than stock. Nothing a free ride does is kept: it writes no record. A setting, laid over the stored one. |
| `minutes`  | ...and its LENGTH row, for a tricks run: `2`, `4` or `6` — how long the clock gives before the buzzer.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `craft`    | Which craft: `skiff`, `marlin`, `otter` or `dart`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `scene`    | A staged moment from `pwa/src/game/scenarios.ts` (`cruise`, `carve`, `brake`, `chop`, `launch`, `landing`, `dive`, `ocean`, `backflip`…) for labs and shots. It stands the craft at the moment and takes the LIGHTS off in front of it, so a staged run is never counted in — and it is not an honest run, so nothing it does is booked. It lasts as long as the page load that names it and is **never stored**: no card carries a row to take one off again, so a kept scene would silently re-stage every ride afterwards.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| `t`        | Seconds into the scene to stand at.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `shot`     | `1` freezes the frame for the screenshot tool and sets `window.__SH_READY__` when it is drawn.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `wind`     | A wind speed, m/s, in place of the level's own (from the same quarter) — the sea is grown from it too. Like `scene`, it is a developer OVERRIDE: it lasts as long as the page load that names it, is never stored, and a run carrying it is not written into any book.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `hs`       | A sea quoted by its significant height, m, in place of the one the wind grows: `hs=20` is the storm the model is sized to carry. Like `scene`, it is a developer OVERRIDE: it lasts as long as the page load that names it, is never stored, and a run carrying it is not written into any book.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `hour`     | An hour on the clock (solar time, 0–24) to START at in place of the level's own, so any seed can be ridden at sunrise or sunset. The clock runs on from there at an hour of sun a minute of riding; the sun, the sky and what the water reflects follow it, and the sea does not.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `time`     | The FREE ride's TIME row — a race, a time trial and a tricks run ride the day their shore deals, so on those a link's `time`, `season`, `day`, `waves` and `weather` are the lab's overrides and no row offers them: `sunrise`, `day`, `sunset` or `night`, resolved by the engine against this coast's own daylight window (R13) in the season being ridden, rather than as hours written down. `night` is the one rung outside that window — midday's opposite on the clock, so the sun at its lowest and the stars fully out — and the one no seed is ever dealt. `hour` wins where both are given.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `season`   | The start card's SEASON row: `spring`, `summer`, `autumn` or `winter` — the sun's arc, and so how long the day is and how dark the night gets. A taiga summer night never gets past twilight; an autumn one is black under the moon. Moves the sun only: the water's temperature and what swims in it stay the level's own.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `day`      | The start card's WIND row, m/s: the figure itself (`day=33`), anywhere from a flat calm to 40 m/s, or one of the three rungs by name — `fine` (4), `windy` (12) or `storm` (20) — which is the wind, the sea the fetch law grows out of it, and the sky that belongs over that wind. It is FREE's row, so a measured run on a pinned shore ignores it and rides the shore's own wind. `wind` and `hs` each win over it, being the developer's own exact figures.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `waves`    | The start card's WAVES row (R36): how big the GROUNDSWELL standing off the coast is, in metres of significant height. FREE's fader stands anywhere inside the engine's own `SWELL_DIAL`, and the Douglas scale's bands — `1`, `2.5`, `4`, `6`, `9`, `14`, `20` — are the words a figure is READ as on a level's box. It is not the wind's sea and `day` does not move it: it is days old and a thousand kilometres away. It is a BASELINE rather than a ceiling — the open ocean past the level's rim still builds its own storm on top of it.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `weather`  | The start card's WEATHER row (FREE's, like the two above it): a sky (`clear`, `haze`, `high`, `overcast`, `rain`, `squall` — the ones this coast offers) in place of the one the wind implies. The sea stays the wind's, so a squall asked for this way is a squall's light over whatever sea is running.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `camera`   | The rung of the camera ladder a run opens on: `bow`, `nose`, `close`, `chase`, `far`, `heli` or `drone`. The camera key still walks the whole ladder from there. Named so a shot, a bug report or a link can stand on one view — `scripts/screenshot.mjs`'s `--camera` names the file after it, which is how the ladder is judged rung against rung.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| `water`    | The picture's WATER row: `low`, `medium` or `high` — THE WHOLE SEA as one word. The surface itself (how fine the near water grid is, how far out it reaches, how far the shader's ripples survive — the one half of the page whose bill is CPU rather than pixels) and everything drawn on it: the spray off the hull, the wake behind it (foam alone, or the water bending for the craft as well), what a hull going in does to the sea (nothing, the crater it knocks, or the ring wave rolling out of it too), and whether the shore and the craft stand mirrored in it. The four are the stop's expansion rather than settings of their own, so `?water=low` is a low sea in every part of itself.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `res`      | The picture's RESOLUTION row: `low`, `medium` or `high` — what share of the device's own pixels the frame is drawn at, under the page's `MAX_DPR` cap.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `detail`   | The picture's DETAIL row: `low`, `medium` or `high` — everything AROUND the water, as one choice: the sea life under it, how thickly the shore is planted, how many sheets of cloud are in the sky (and so in the sea reflecting them), and how much rain is in the air and whether it lands on the water. Nothing on this row touches what the sea itself is drawn like — that is `water`'s, entire, so `detail=high&water=low` is a thick shore over a cheap sea.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `distance` | The picture's DISTANCE row: `low`, `medium` or `high` — how much of the coast is drawn, how far out its cover stands, how far the SEA itself is drawn as a sea rather than as the long swell alone (a ring of near water a stop, each doubling the reach), and how hard the fog is pulled in to close over the cut. Nothing it stops drawing was visible: every stop thickens the air until the haze has shut before the world ends.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| `see`      | `0` closes the water's window: the near sea is solid at every angle and nothing under it — the bed, a rock's foot, a school — is drawn. `1` opens it.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `fps`      | The picture's FRAME RATE row: `30`, `60` or `max` — the most frames a second the loop draws. `max` is the display's own rate; a cap under it draws fewer frames on a schedule the machine can keep, and the engine steps the same seconds behind whichever are drawn.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `start`    | `1` skips both cards and rides — a pinned run.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `paused`   | `1` rides one and holds it under the pause card straight away, which is how that surface is photographed and how a report about it is handed on. Implies a run, so `start` is not needed beside it.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `splash`   | `0` clears the attract card off an ordinary visit (what the developer page's repro links carry); `1` forces it back on.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `menu`     | Open the front door ON a page: `root`, `campaign` (the ladder), `levels` (which pinned shore a race, a tricks run or a time trial is on), `start` (the seed and the day, FREE's own), `craft`, `options`, `keys` (the bindings, behind OPTIONS), `developer`, `unlocks` (the campaign's shores opened or shut by hand, behind DEVELOPER) or `benchHistory` (the benchmark's list, behind DEVELOPER). The last three let the developer menu out with them — a URL that names a page has, by definition, found it. `campaign` and `levels` both open on their COAST step, the row of shore banners; there is no parameter for a shore's own grid, which is one press past it.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `probe`    | `0` skips the first-visit probe on this visit — the two seconds under the attract card that measure the machine and may hand an untouched picture the HIGH stops of WATER, DETAIL and DISTANCE. The labs carry it so a row never moves under the camera; a link has no reason to.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `update`   | `1` shows the new-build button as if a newer build were waiting — the only way to look at it before a deploy has landed on a device that already had the app. It stands over a run and over the front door alike, in the same bottom-right corner. The second press reloads the page.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |

A URL that names a RUN (`start`, `scene`, `shot`, `paused`) boots into one, past the attract card and the front door. Anything else opens the front door, with the URL's seed, craft, time, day, weather and picture rows as the settings it is standing on — so a link decides what RIDE rides without deciding that it has already been pressed. Every one of these except `update` is a row on the start card, the level card, the craft card, the options page or the developer page. COPY REPRO LINK writes back what decides the RUN — the seed, the craft, the day and the developer's own overrides; the picture rows are a fact about the machine reading the link rather than about the frame, so they are left to whoever opens it. `scripts/screenshot.mjs` and `scripts/profile-render.mjs` both take them as flags (`--water`, `--res`, `--detail`, `--see`, `--fps`), which is how a picture ladder is looked at and metered one stop at a time; the screenshot tool takes `--camera` the same way.

The debug switch the spec asks for (§19.3) is the dev build: `npm run dev` lifts the engine's `debug`-level output onto the console through `pwa/src/output-bridge.ts`, and the same lines are kept in an in-memory ring buffer every build can read back.

## What the game remembers

Everything the player chooses — the craft, the shore's seed, the hour to ride it at, the wind and the sky to ride it under, the camera, the sound fader, the vibration switch, which key does what, whether the HUD is drawn and whether it shows the frame rate, the four picture rows, and the developer page's own frame-cost toggle once the menu has been let out — is kept in `localStorage` under `sea-haven-settings` and read back through `mergeSettings` (`pwa/src/game/settings.ts`). The merge is field by field and every value is checked against what the build still offers, so a blob written by an older build keeps the choices that still exist and quietly drops the ones that do not. Clearing site data is a first visit again; storage being unavailable is a session on the defaults, which is a perfectly good game.

**The campaign's board.** What every level of the campaign has paid the whole field, and the player's best on each — the figure, the hull, the place, the medal — is kept under `sea-haven-campaign` (`pwa/src/game/campaign.ts`), read back through `mergeProgress` with the same field-by-field check: a level the ladder no longer has, a hull the roster no longer carries or a medal that is not one of the three is dropped rather than kept as a ghost on the table. The board keeps the better afternoon of every level and is never spent by a worse one.

**The record book.** The best this machine has seen on each shore in each mode — a time for a race or a time trial, a score for a tricks run, and the hull that set it — is kept beside the settings under `sea-haven-records` (`pwa/src/game/records.ts`). A row is named by the LEVEL (the coast, the seed, the track kind and the class the course was paced for, plus a tricks run's length) and the mode, and by nothing the rider chose on the same water: not the craft, not the hour, not the wind. It is beaten outright or not at all — a tie is a tie — and only by an honest run: a staged scene, a developer's wind or sea, or the bot riding under a card posts nothing. That test governs the BOOKS alone — a rider who crossed the line still gets the finish plate, it simply claims nothing and never says NEW BEST. The level card shows the row on each shore's box; the result plate at the end of a run says NEW BEST when the book gained one.

**The ghost's tape.** A TRICKS run and a TIME TRIAL are ridden alone, so each one keeps your best run on that shore as the CONTROLS you rode it on, and puts it back out there the next time you ride it: a see-through craft riding the shore again, exactly as you rode it, with the gap to it beside the clock. The water is always one of the campaign's pinned levels — those three modes ride the level card rather than a seed — so a tape is kept under `sea-haven-ghost:<mode>/<level>`, one key per level per mode, and reading the ghost for the shore about to be ridden never means parsing every tape on the machine (`pwa/src/game/ghost.ts`). A RACE and a CAMPAIGN rung keep none: both have eleven other hulls on the water, and a rival's wake is real water the hull reads, so that run cannot be ridden again without riding the whole field again. It is a recording of button presses rather than a path: the engine is deterministic, so the same shore, the same hull and the same inputs put the craft over the same metre of water, and a two-minute run costs a few tens of kilobytes. A tape is kept on the run that BEAT the figure on file — or on any run at all where there is no readable tape for that shore yet — and it is dropped rather than risked when it would be too big for the store or when the shore it was cut on has moved under it (every tape carries the level's own fingerprint). Tapes for water the game no longer has — a level off the ladder, a key an older build wrote — are swept the next time the game starts. Nothing about it is load-bearing: a machine with no storage still rides, and the record still stands.

**The replay, which is the one thing NOT kept.** Every RACE, TRICKS run and TIME TRIAL is recorded as you ride it — the same kind of tape the ghost is, the controls the engine was handed plus the moments the run marked as worth a camera — and it is offered on the finish plate and on the pause card. It is held in memory alone: nothing about a replay is written to `localStorage`, to IndexedDB or anywhere else, it lives as long as the tab does, and leaving one drops it (`pwa/src/game/replay.ts`). That is a deliberate choice rather than a gap. A run's worth to the player is already written down in the record book and the campaign's board; a replay is worth watching once, and a roll of them would be the one thing in this game large enough to make a browser start asking about storage.

**The first visit measures the machine.** A browser on a phone reports every GPU as the same string and no model, so the game cannot look a device up; instead, on a visit that has never been measured, it draws the design point under the attract card for a couple of seconds with the GPU drained after every frame, and a machine whose worst ordinary frame would still fit inside the display's own period at two and a half times the cost is handed the HIGH stops of WATER, DETAIL and DISTANCE before OPTIONS is ever opened (`pwa/src/game/video-probe.ts` owns the rule, `tests/video_test.ts` holds it). The verdict is written to the stored settings either way (`probed`), so nobody is measured twice, and the promotion only ever touches a picture with every row at its default — a rider who has been to OPTIONS has said what they think, and a blob from an older build keeps its choices. RESTORE DEFAULTS clears the flag with the rest, which is the way to be measured again; `?probe=0` skips the measuring for one visit.

The **developer menu** is let out by holding START on the front door for seven seconds, and it stays out. RESTORE DEFAULTS on the options page deliberately leaves it out; LOCK THE DEVELOPER MENU on the developer page is the way back.

**The campaign's board, set by hand.** DEVELOPER ▸ UNLOCKS opens or shuts the campaign's shores — one at a time, or the whole ladder at once — by writing the same board a finished run writes (`sea-haven-campaign`, `pwa/src/game/campaign.ts`). Both presses work on a prefix, so the ladder can never be left with a shore standing open behind one that has never been ridden. A granted level carries a win and no figure, since nobody rode it; the **record book** under `sea-haven-records` is not touched by either press.

**The benchmark's history.** Every run DEVELOPER ▸ BENCHMARK has scored on this machine is kept beside the settings under `sea-haven-benchmarks` (`pwa/src/game/benchmark-history.ts`), newest first, twenty deep. A record is the whole RUN and not its score — every reading, what each of those frames cost the renderer, and what was standing in the scene on the last of them — because what the list is for is getting a run BACK: the graph redrawn, the sag found at the frame it happened on, the debug report copied out weeks later. It carries the conditions with it, OPTIONS ▸ VIDEO as it stood included, since the whole method is running the benchmark twice with one picture row moved and reading the difference. Being per machine is the point: a phone and a desktop are a factor of ten apart on exactly this work. The page's own CLEAR is the way to empty it, and a full or unavailable store costs the history rather than the run.

## Installing

Every dependency resolves from the public npm registry, so `npm install` needs no token and no `~/.npmrc` entry. Claude web sessions run `.claude/hooks/session-start.sh`, which installs and builds in the background so the tooling is ready when the session opens.

## Build-time environment

| Variable                           | Meaning                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `VITE_BASE`                        | Deploy base path: `/` (default), `/preview/`, `/branch/`. Drives the SW scope, the manifest identity, and every emitted URL.                                   |
| `VITE_PWA_IGNORE_PATHS`            | Comma-separated absolute paths the built service worker must NOT claim. Only the root slot sets it (`/preview/,/branch/`) so nested slots own their own pages. |
| `GITHUB_SHA` / `GITHUB_RUN_NUMBER` | Provided by CI; baked into the build label the HUD corner shows.                                                                                               |
| `CHROMIUM_PATH`                    | The browser the screenshot and profile tools drive. Claude web sessions have one at `/opt/pw-browsers/chromium`.                                               |

`.env.example` at the root documents the same set; copy it to `.env` (gitignored) to override locally.

## The desktop shell's environment

Read by the Tauri app's own binary (`tauri/`, see [platforms.md](platforms.md)), never by the website's build. All optional — with none of them set the app serves the copy of the site bundled inside it.

| Variable       | Meaning                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `SH_GAME_URL`  | Load a remote URL instead of the bundled site (e.g. `https://game3.niclaslindstedt.se/preview/`), for debugging the shell against live content. |
| `SH_WEBROOT`   | Serve the site from another directory without rebuilding the app.                                                                               |
| `SH_VERBOSE=1` | Keep the informational log lines in a release build (a debug build prints them anyway). Warnings and errors are never suppressed.               |

Packaging reads one more: `APPLE_SIGNING_IDENTITY`, the Developer ID a macOS build is signed with. Absent, the app is signed ad hoc — enough to run on Apple Silicon, at the cost of one Gatekeeper prompt the release notes explain.

On a runner nobody sets it by hand: `.github/actions/apple-signing` imports a certificate into a throwaway keychain and reads the identity back out of it, so a renewed certificate is one secret to replace rather than two to keep agreeing. Five repository secrets, all optional:

| Secret                        | Meaning                                                                                     |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `MAC_CSC_LINK`                | The Developer ID Application certificate as a base64-encoded `.p12` (`base64 -i cert.p12`). |
| `MAC_CSC_KEY_PASSWORD`        | The password that `.p12` was exported with.                                                 |
| `APPLE_ID`                    | The Apple ID the notarization request is made as.                                           |
| `APPLE_APP_SPECIFIC_PASSWORD` | An app-specific password for it, from appleid.apple.com.                                    |
| `APPLE_TEAM_ID`               | The ten-character Developer Team ID the certificate belongs to.                             |

The first two sign; the last three notarize, and the bundler acts on them only once the app carries a real signature — so half a set signs without notarizing rather than failing. `MAC_SIGN_IDENTITY` overrides the identity read out of the certificate, and is needed only where the keychain holds more than one.

Every launch is written to `launch.log` in the app's own user-data directory — `%APPDATA%\seahaven` on Windows, `~/Library/Application Support/seahaven` on macOS, `~/.local/share/seahaven` on Linux — with the previous launch kept beside it as `launch.log.prev`. The window's remembered geometry (`window-state.json`) is there too. The player's settings are NOT: those are the webview's own origin-keyed storage, exactly as in a browser.

## The store app's environment

The store app (`native/`) has an environment of its own, documented in [`native/.env.example`](../native/.env.example) and read neither by the website's build nor by the desktop shell — `APPLE_TEAM_ID` names the same Apple team in both, and is the one value the two have in common. Every value in it is either a credential or a personal identifier, so all of them come from the environment rather than from a committed file; the one that changes what the app DOES is:

| Variable               | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `EXPO_PUBLIC_GAME_URL` | Point the app's WebView at a deployed slot instead of the copy of the site bundled inside it. **Local debugging only** — a store build that streams the website is the exact shape App Store guideline 4.2 rejects, so it is unset in every profile in `eas.json`. |

The rest (`EXPO_TOKEN`, `EAS_PROJECT_ID`, `APPLE_TEAM_ID`, the App Store Connect key, the Play service account) authenticate a build or a submission and are covered by [`native/RELEASING.md`](../native/RELEASING.md).

## The deploy slots

`pages.yml` builds three whole sites and merges them into one Pages artifact served at `game3.niclaslindstedt.se` (the custom domain in `pwa/public/CNAME`; DNS is a CNAME on `niclaslindstedt.github.io`, and the repo's Pages settings must say "GitHub Actions" + that domain):

- `/` — the highest `v*` tag (or `main` before the first release), with `VITE_PWA_IGNORE_PATHS` set so its service worker disowns the nested slots.
- `/preview/` — the triggering `main` commit, every push.
- `/branch/` — parked by `workflow_dispatch` with a `branch_ref` input; persisted in the `branch-deploy` orphan branch so ordinary deploys carry it forward until the next dispatch overwrites it.

Each slot's manifest gets a distinct `id`/`scope`/`start_url` and install name, so side-by-side installs don't collide.

## Releases

`version-bump.yml` (manual dispatch, and the only entry point) checks the branch and the tree, prints the version it is about to cut, and calls `release.yml`, which derives the bump from `.changes/unreleased/` fragments, rewrites every version string via `scripts/update-versions.sh`, collates the CHANGELOG, commits `chore(release): vX.Y.Z`, tags, creates the GitHub Release as a DRAFT, packages the desktop downloads on a runner per platform and attaches them, publishes the release only once all three have landed, and chains into `pages.yml` so `/` serves the new tag immediately. It is one dispatched run under the default `GITHUB_TOKEN` — no `RELEASE_TOKEN` PAT, because no cross-workflow trigger is needed (both workflow headers say why). Preview locally with `make bump` and `make changelog VERSION=X.Y.Z`.

## Identity

Name, copy, palette, and URLs live in `pwa/src/identity.ts` and nowhere else; `pwa/index.html` (the static head — deliberately carrying no crawlable copy, see _Discoverability_), `pwa/public/` (robots/CNAME, the privacy and support pages), and the icon generator all follow it. Changing identity means touching those in the same change — AGENTS.md's parity table is the checklist.

## Discoverability

**The web deploy is not indexed, on purpose.** The site is the game's free, incomplete cut; it is not meant to be found through a search engine or to unfurl as a card in a chat client, so it emits none of the signals that would make either work:

| Surface                             | What it carries                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `pwa/index.html` head               | `noindex,nofollow,noarchive,nosnippet,noimageindex` and nothing else a crawler reads: no meta description, no canonical, no sitemap link, no Open Graph or Twitter card, no JSON-LD |
| `pwa/index.html` body               | `#root` and a `<noscript>` line. No prerendered copy describing the game                                                                                                            |
| `pwa/public/robots.txt`             | `Disallow: /`, advertising no sitemap                                                                                                                                               |
| `sitemap.xml`, `llms.txt`, `og.png` | not shipped, and not generated                                                                                                                                                      |
| `privacy/`, `support/`              | still REACHABLE — a store review fetches them by URL — but `noindex,nofollow` and no canonical                                                                                      |

`robots.txt` alone would only ask a crawler not to fetch; a URL it already knows can still be listed. The per-page `noindex` is what covers that, which is why both are there. `tests/identity_test.ts` holds all of it: adding a discovery tag, a crawler file or prerendered body copy back fails the suite.

This is a deliberate deviation from OSS_GAME_SPEC §11.3, recorded as such in [spec-conformance.md](spec-conformance.md).

## Losing focus

A hidden tab, a minimised window or a phone call **pauses the run** — the run clock included — and coming back lands on the very frame it left (OSS_GAME_SPEC §37.3). This is a single-player game, so there is nobody the world has to keep moving for. The decision lives in `pwa/src/game/run-loop.ts`, which also clamps a long frame to a tenth of a second and drops the time beyond it rather than simulating a stall (§37.2).
