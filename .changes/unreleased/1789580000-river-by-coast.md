title: Each coast draws its own river, with banks of its own
---

A river is now the coast's: the mangrove's leaves through a mouth opened wider than the race's channel, holds its width a long way up the country in loops half as wide again, runs at a fraction of the taiga's current, and drops two to four low bars in its mouth — a delta a rider threads between — where the taiga's stays the rock channel it was, closing fast to a creek and running hard. The ground beside every river past its mouth is now a BANK rather than the coast's own quilt: soil and grass down to the water with the last metre stripped to stone (mud on the mangrove), and the alder, the willow, the birch and the mangroves grow on it. How steeply a coast's land comes down to the water is a knob on its row too, though both built coasts keep the rule book's own rise.
