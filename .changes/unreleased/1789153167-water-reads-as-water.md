title: The sea no longer reads as woven cloth
---

The wind ripples on the water were drawn from seven directional sines over a
noise that did not repeat across its own tile. The two loudest sines crossed
into a regular interference lattice, and the noise left a step at the tile's
edge that came out as a ridge of slope — so the sea carried a woven twill with
a hard grid ruled across it every couple of metres, and a second grid at the
coarse layer's spacing, turned. The tile is now transformed out of the sea's
own spectrum with a random phase on every component: nothing dominates,
nothing lines up, and it wraps by construction.

Two things behind the light were counted wrong with it. The glint's lobe was
handed the ripples' share of the sea's roughness back by a factor that tracked
the WIND instead of the distance, so the near water was rough twice over; and
the two ripple layers were mixed without normalising, so the tile delivered
half the slope it claimed. The sun's and the moon's road on the water is also
elliptical now, the way Cox and Munk measured it — a sea tilts more along the
wind than across it, so the road is drawn out along the wind rather than laid
down as a disc.
