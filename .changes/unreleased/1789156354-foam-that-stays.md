title: Foam that stays in the water
---

The sea's whitecaps were flashes rather than foam. What the water drew was read off the surface AT THAT INSTANT — how steep this face is, how high this crest stands — and a patch of water is at the top of a wavelet for about a tenth of a second: measured on seed 28, the median spell a point spent foamed was 0.13 s and not one of them reached three tenths. The sea came out sprinkled with sparks that appeared and vanished before the eye could read them as anything.

Foam belongs to the water, not to the wave that made it: it sits where the crest left it while the wave rolls on, and dies over seconds. It does that now — what is breaking is sown into a field anchored to the world and decays at a whitecap's own rate, so a breaking crest lays a patch of white and leaves it behind. The same spell is now 1.1–2.4 s. And the whitecaps are blown by the wind THIS water feels rather than the level's headline: a bay in the lee of a headland is glassy for the same reason its water is flat.
