title: A jump length counter, and points for the metres
---

A jump is two measurements and the game only ever took one. Beside the air
clock over the nose there is now a **jump length** in metres — how far the
flight carried you, from the point the hull left the water — and the score
pays it. It is paid by the metre on the air's own rising curve, tied to it at
the speed an ordinary ramp actually carries a rider (measured: 17.5 m in the
air's own one-second knee), so a typical jump earns as much for where it went
as for how long it hung there, and an ordinary jump is now worth twice what
its seconds alone were worth.

The two halves only come apart where they should. A hull popped straight up
off a crest is all seconds and no metres; one driven flat off a lip at speed
is the other way about. The run keeps its longest hang and its longest carry
as separate records, because they are two different jumps as often as they
are one, and each takes the **RECORD** word on its own half of the readout.
