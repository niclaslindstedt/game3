title: How big the waves are is now a knob
---

Every shore is dealt a GROUNDSWELL of its own — the sea that has piled up out past the coast, raised by weather days old and a thousand kilometres away, from a slight metre to a phenomenal twenty — and the start card's new WAVES row asks for any height on the scale instead. It is not the wind row in different words: the wind builds the chop beside you now, the swell owes it nothing, and a flat blue morning with ten metres rolling under it is a day the card can finally ask for. It is a baseline rather than a ceiling, so turning your back on the course and holding the throttle open still builds the sea the whole way out, whatever the shore was dealt. A link carries it as `?waves=`.
