title: A race spends its processor on the riders you can actually see
---

Every rider on the water had his whole figure re-solved and re-emitted sixty times a second — twelve men in a race, most of them behind you or a hundred metres off. A rival's figure is now rebuilt only while he can be read: inside the lens or the water's reflection of it, and near enough that a shoulder rolling into a turn is more than a pixel. The man you are looking at is never affected, and every rider's body goes on answering the hull whether or not he is on screen, so one coming back into view is posed from a body that never stopped rather than snapping into place.
