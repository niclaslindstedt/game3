title: The two closest cameras, at night
---

The bow and handlebar views both sit BEHIND the craft's own lamps, so at night the headlamp's bloom hung across the frame as a flare, its lens burned in the middle of the picture, and the two rail lamps filled the bottom corners as coloured slabs — worst from the bow, where the lamp stands half a metre in front of the lens. The craft now puts that hardware away whenever the frame is drawn from a seat on it; the beam stays lit, so the pool it lays on the water is unchanged.

The rail lamps were also on the wrong sides as the rider sees them — green to port, red to starboard — and now read the way every small boat's do.

Both of those views also banked with the hull hard enough to tip the horizon through a turn. They still lean, by about half as much: the hull rolls in the frame, the sea stays where it is.
