type: Fixed
title: No wake on dry land
---

A craft that runs up the shore no longer goes on churning the sea. The pump's stream, the brake's pool and a capsized hull's boil are all laid from the craft's own state rather than from the trail it leaves, so nothing stopped them when the hull left the water — a rider parked eight metres up a hillside with the throttle open was still writing white water and, since the surface now bends for the trail, lifting a sea that was not under them. A flight that ends on the beach no longer throws a landing plume or stamps a crater and a ring wave either.
