title: The air clock arrives quietly, grows with the flight, and holds a record
---

The air time over the nose no longer pops into the frame the instant the hull leaves the water: it FADES up, which is a reading rather than a flinch at the moment a rider has the least attention to spare. Then it grows as the flight goes on — half again as big by a flight worth the whole size — so the shape of the thing already says whether this one is big before a digit has been read.

Take the run's longest flight and it STICKS: the clock stops on the number flown and holds it for a couple of seconds after the landing, pulsing, with RECORD under it — under, so the number never moves off the centreline to make room for the news. The word arrives with a chime of its own, four thin sines climbing a major triad, pitched clear of the splash it plays over.
