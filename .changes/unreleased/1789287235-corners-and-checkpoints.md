---
type: Changed
title: Corners a craft can hold, and one lit checkpoint at a time
---

The generator no longer draws corners the roster cannot be steered round. R23's floor on how tight the racing line may turn rises from 55 m to 80 m — measured off the craft rather than argued, because the number a hull SETTLES at in a full-lock circle leaves out the seconds it takes to get there, and the fastest craft in the catalog needs 68 m of ground down its entry heading to come round a right angle at all. The ocean leg's rounding, which was drawn UNDER the old floor and was the tightest corner of the course on nine levels out of ten, is now a wide mark to lean through instead of a wall to stop for. And a new rule, R34, holds the corner a rider actually meets: the turn from one gate's approach to its exit, which R23's reading of the LINE cannot see when a whole bend falls between one gate and the next. Over eighty levels the worst gate used to ask for 112°; it now asks for 68°.

Only the checkpoint you owe is lit. A gate's lanterns come on the instant the gate before it is crossed and go out as you cross it, so there is one lit pair of marks on the water instead of a chain of them running off down the coast — a target rather than a map. The paint still says which gates are ahead and which are done.

And a checkpoint is only taken by going through it. Crossing a gate's line outside its buoys no longer counts as having reached it: you pay for a gate you went past at the next one you actually thread, and if you go past several you have to come back for one.
