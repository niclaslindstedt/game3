type: Changed
title: The camera has weight, and the engine is heard through the water
---

The chase camera's boom is carried on a mass rather than a lag. It swings onto the flight path as the craft leaves the water and, because a mass cannot be stopped, it swings back THROUGH level at the landing — dipping the lens a little under its natural angle before it settles, so hitting the water bounces the shot instead of snapping it. The old boom stopped dead the frame a probe touched water; measured, that one frame moved the lens thirty-seven times as hard as the whole rest of the flight.

And the engine now sounds like what it is: a machine whose exhaust exits below the waterline. For nearly the whole of a run it is heard through water — quiet, dark, and carried by the wet blat at the pipe, which now tears rather than being blown clear as the revs rise. The moment the hull comes clear, over a wave or off a ramp, the note brightens and the exhaust's edge cracks open.
