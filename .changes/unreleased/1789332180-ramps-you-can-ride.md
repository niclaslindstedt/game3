title: No ramp in a tricks run faces the way you are riding
---

The trick field lays ramps down the shore and back up it, and a ramp is ridden from one side and met from the other as a wall. The homebound half was standing on the same water as the outbound half, so riding out you met every other deck lip-first — a thing in the way rather than a lip you chose to leave alone. The homebound line now stands off to seaward, and any pair the step does not separate — two outbound decks facing each other across a hairpin, say — is refused outright. The rule that keeps a trick ramp across the sea was also inert, and works again.
