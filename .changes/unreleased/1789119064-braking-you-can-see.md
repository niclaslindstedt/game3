title: Braking you can see
---

Pulling the brake used to look like nothing at all. The boil the reverse bucket throws was born at the keel, and a hull under its bucket sits its keel a quarter of a metre under the surface, so the white water never broke it — and with the pump's flow going forward the road behind only paled, so from the saddle the craft simply slowed for no visible reason.

Now the bucket shows on every surface it touches. The boil is born at the sea's own surface and thrown higher and further forward the faster the water is rushing past, two wings of broken white either side of the hull; the water shader lays a pool of foam and churn under the hull, wider than it and reaching past the bow at pace, and the road it leaves behind is as white as a driven one's and wider; and the rev bar's caption reads BRAKE — REVERSE once the craft is backing up — in the touch lever's own brake colour, so a rider on the keys, who has no lever to watch, reads it off the corner they already read the speed from.
