Two things quietly took the groundswell away from a run that had asked for one. The WAVES row set to twenty metres over a WIND row set to nothing gave a mirror, because a wind of zero is how the engine spells a flat calm and it took the swell with it — now a swell the rider ASKED for stands in that calm, which is the day R36 describes, while the one a coast was dealt still goes with its wind. And turning the WIND FROM row off the land flattened the swell along with the sea the wind grows, because the swell's share of the coast was measured up the wind; it is measured dead onshore now, since the weather that made a groundswell is a thousand kilometres away and owes today's wind nothing.
