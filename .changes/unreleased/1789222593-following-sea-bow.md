title: A buried bow is no longer braked as though it were twice the hull
---

Two things in the hull model only went wrong on the face of a wave, and both cost a bow that had gone in. The drag on a buried section grew with depth without limit, so a hull a metre under was charged twice the frontal area it has and braked at eleven g. And the impact that throws a bow back out was read against the world's vertical, so a hull driving into the back of the wave ahead in a following sea — the water under it falling away while the face rose into its path — read as receding from water it was closing on at five metres a second, and nothing fired. The buried hull is now charged for the section it actually has, and the bow feels the face it is actually meeting.
