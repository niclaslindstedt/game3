title: Lit buoys to round, and the light they throw on the water
---

The corners of an ocean circuit are marked by BUOYS: moored steel cans riding the swell, yellow with a black band, a lattice tower over them and a lantern four metres up. Two to four of them stand on a lap and at least one is out in the open sea, so every lap includes a run out to round something and back. Each carries its own light CHARACTER the way a chart quotes one — a single flash, or a group of two, three or four, every four to nine seconds — and no two on a lap flash alike, so a rider picks the corner ahead out of a black sea by counting them. The lamps light the water round themselves: a pool on the sea and a column of it down the ripples that pulses with the flash, which is what makes a rounding mark findable at dusk, through the night and into the dawn. Nothing about a lamp is stepped or stored — it is a pure function of the level's own clock, so a run replays the light it was ridden under exactly.
