title: The arcade's hand in the air
---

Jumps now land the way an arcade game's jumps should. Over the last moment before the water the game asks where the hull will be pointing when it hits, and if the answer is on its side or on its nose it turns the craft toward the attitude it ought to land at — so a launch off a lip the sea kicked, or a hang the air wound nose-up, is a landing ridden away from instead of a swim. Measured over a grid of staged launches with the rider's hands still, bad landings fall from two in three to under one in twelve.

It never touches a flight that was going to be fine, so a clean jump feels exactly as it did; and the air is still the rider's — hold the lean, either way, and you get the bare physics, which is how a backflip still comes round and how you can still stuff the nose on purpose. How much of the hand a run is ridden with is a dial, ready for a difficulty setting to move.
