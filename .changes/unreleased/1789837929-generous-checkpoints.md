type: Changed
title: A checkpoint counts if any part of you went through it
---

Checkpoints are judged against the whole machine now instead of against the
craft's centre of gravity. A rail that went past the buoy, a helmet that went
up through the hoop, a hull thrown broadside across a ring — any of them takes
the gate, and on top of that sits half a metre of leeway nobody can see at
gate range. A crossing too close to call is one you are given, so the five
seconds a miss costs are only ever charged for a checkpoint the rider was
shown going by.

The berth is wider, not the rule looser: which side of a rounding buoy you
went is still a decision and gets no leeway at all, the finish must still be
crossed through its opening, and a hull passing clear over a ring is still
judged by its keel rather than by the rider standing above it. Across the
bot's sweep it is about one missed checkpoint in six handed back.
