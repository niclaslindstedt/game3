A start card between the front door and the water: START now asks what the run is before standing it up. Four rows — which craft, which shore (a seed, with the whole course charted under it: the shoreline, the shallows, the skerries and the line through the gates, cut from the real generated level), what time of day (sunrise, day or sunset, worked out from the coast's own daylight rather than from three hours written down), and what weather (fine, wind or storm, one word covering the sky and the sea under it) — and every one of them can be left AS DEALT to ride the level exactly as it was generated. The chart is built in a worker, so the sea behind the card never stops moving while a seed is walked, and a seed already looked at comes back instantly. All four rows travel as a URL (`?seed=`, `?craft=`, `?time=`, `?day=`), so a shore somebody found is a link.
