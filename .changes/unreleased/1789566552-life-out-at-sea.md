title: Life out at sea
---

The open water past the course is no longer empty. The big animals now live
where they belong — out where the shelf falls away — and a dozen new ones ride
with them: the grey seal, the cod and the salmon shoals, the white-beaked
dolphins, the basking shark and the humpback on the cold coast; the spotted
dolphin schools, the tiger shark, the manta, the whale shark and a rorqual of
its own on the warm one. Each coast also flies birds that never come ashore —
gannets, fulmars and kittiwakes on one, boobies, noddies and sooty terns on
the other — so there is something in the sky over the outer half of a level as
well as in the water under it. A cold sea is no longer a dead one either: the
coldest third of the northern coast's seeds used to carry almost nothing, and
now carries the whales.

One side effect worth knowing about: a seed's default groundswell was being
dealt from the same stream the sea life is drawn off, one draw downstream of
it, so it moved whenever an animal did. The sea life now has a stream of its
own and the swell can never be re-rolled by a whale again — but that fix
shifts the swell a seed is dealt once, this release, on roughly half of them.
An explicitly asked-for swell height is unaffected.
