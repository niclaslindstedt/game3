title: Waves that stand up in the world
---

The sea has shape and scale. Every wave now carries Stokes' second-order crest — a peaked top over a long flat trough, the shape water actually has, instead of a rounded sinusoid — and the wind sea is half again as tall as the fetch law alone grows it. Out past the coastal shelf the bed goes on falling to sixty metres, the open water a storm swell needs to stand its whole height in, and a new `storm` staged moment rides one out there. A sea quoted by its height (`?hs=`) is a young storm sea now rather than a mature ocean swell: twenty metres of significant height arrives as a twelve-second wall you climb, not the five-hundred-metre roller with a ten-degree face that nature would send.
