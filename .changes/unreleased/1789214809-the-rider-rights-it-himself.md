type: Changed
title: The rider rights it himself, and the sea comes off both of them
---

Going over is no longer a hull that swallows its rider and hands him back upright a moment later. He stays on the machine through all of it — sat in the saddle, turned over with it, hands on the bars — and he works at getting it back: his weight thrown at the side the hull has to come back toward, rocked at rather than held, for as long as the haul takes. The haul itself is half a second longer and no longer runs at a constant rate: the hull hangs while he gets to work, comes over fastest as it passes its own beam, and settles the last few degrees instead of stopping dead.

And the sea comes off the pair of them while it happens, where before there was one splash at the end: a sheet off the flank being carried up over the top, the water running off the man as the hull turns him back out of the sea, the burst where he comes through the surface, and the slap of that raised flank coming down flat — with its own crater — as the hull finally levels.
