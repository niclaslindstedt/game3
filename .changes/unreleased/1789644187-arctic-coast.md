title: The arctic coast, and a winter of ice
---

A third coast to ride: the ARCTIC — a polar shore where the land is a WALL OF GLACIER ICE standing up to forty metres out of black water at the freezing point, with moraine and gravel in the bays between the fronts, bergs grounded off it where the taiga has skerries, and a CRACK in the ice for its river. It has its own year (two seasons under the midnight sun, two under a low sun with dark nights), its own skies (sea smoke for its haze, snow for its rain, a white-out blizzard for its squall), its own cover (nothing with a trunk — purple saxifrage and moss campion in cushions, cotton grass, an inch-high willow, lichen on the stones, kelp under the water and stranded ice on the tideline), its own birds (the glaucous and the ivory gull, little auks whirring low over the sea, murres, puffins and black guillemots, king eiders, the brent and the snow geese going over, a skua, a gyrfalcon and a raven) and its own sea life (polar cod under the ice, capelin on the gravel, char out of the crack, ringed and bearded seals, harp seals in herds, walrus, belugas, a polar bear swimming the lead, the narwhal, the sleeper shark that never comes up, and — one ride in eight — a bowhead).

And in its WINTER the sea is a sheet of ice: an icebreaker has cut the course through it, and a rider who leaves the channel rides up the brash onto the sheet and stops. The water in the channel is a lead's flat chop, the pack runs to the horizon, and the same shore ridden in summer is the same shore without it. `?biome=arctic` is the link, and every lab takes `BIOME=arctic`.
