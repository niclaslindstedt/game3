title: Layered skies, and rain that falls
---

The sky is drawn as real cloud now — sheets of it at the altitudes the cloud
chart puts them, seen in perspective, so a cirrus veil eight kilometres up
drifts over fair-weather cumulus a kilometre above the water and the horizon
fills with cloud instead of a ring of puffs. A clear sky is empty, from one
horizon to the other. Under weather the ceiling is a real ceiling with ragged
scud tearing along beneath it.

Rain and squalls actually rain: a sheet of streaks that leans on the wind and
comes at the visor at speed, thickening with every gust and taking the distance
with it — and it lands, pocking the sea with rings out to a reach the DETAIL
row sets.

And the sea mirrors all of it. The water reflects the very same sky the dome is
painted with, so a squall's black ceiling puts its own black on the water and a
sunset's cloud lies along the crests.
