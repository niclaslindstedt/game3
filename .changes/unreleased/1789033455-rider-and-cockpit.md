title: The rider, and how he sits
---

The rider is built like an athlete instead of a stick — quadriceps and calves, biceps and forearms, a swimmer's back and a rounded seat — drawn a head taller than life so he reads at chase range, and his helmet is a proper racing lid: a wide full-face shell in three bands of livery with a chin bar and a peak. And he now SITS on the machine the way a rider does: the footwells are deep trays sunk into the deck, the saddle stands nearly half a metre over them, the gunwale carries a rail and a lip rather than a hand of trim, and the bars are within a bent arm's reach — so a seated rider cruises upright with his knees below his hips and his elbows down, instead of straddling the deck with his knees up around the bars. The stand-up is ridden the way a stand-up is ridden: feet together on the tray, hips back, bent over the pole.
