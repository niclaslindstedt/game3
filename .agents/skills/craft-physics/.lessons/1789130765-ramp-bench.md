`make ride SCENARIO=launch` stages the run-up already lined up on the
ramp's centreline, so anything that only acts on a hull arriving OFF the
line moves the strip by centimetres and reads as "no effect". The honest
bench is a grid: for each seed and craft, stand the craft a few metres
before the hinge at a spread of across-offsets and heading errors
(`placeRun`), ride with the throttle open and `steer: 0`, and count where
it left the deck — over the lip, or out through a flank (`onRampDeck` is
exported for exactly this). 800 trials over eight seeds and four craft
runs in about two minutes of pure Node.

Two staging traps, both of which read as a broken assist rather than a
broken bench:

- Starting the run-up far out (30 m) lets the heading error carry the
  hull off the deck before it ever gets aboard: half the trials came back
  "never touched the ramp". Start 6–8 m out so the offset and angle you
  asked for are the ones it ARRIVES with.
- `onRampDeck` returns null before the approach lip, so a loop that
  breaks the first time it is null ends on step 1. Latch "has been on the
  deck" (`craft.onRamp`) first, and only then treat null as having left.

The generator's deck is 4 m wide (R8) against a hull 1.1–1.3 m in the
beam, with `contact.rampFriction` 0.08 and no intake in the water — no
thrust, no nozzle, no keel. A hull on a ramp is a sled, so the only
levers are lateral damping and yaw; nothing you do to the hull's water
forces reaches it.

AND THE SAME TRAP IN A STATIC ATTITUDE BENCH. `placeRun({ pitch })` rotates
the craft about the CoG at a height chosen for a LEVEL hull: on a 3.1 m hull
at 40° that puts the transom a metre under, and what comes back is a
submarine's restoring moment, growing with pitch and reading exactly like a
stiff, well-behaved hull. Raise the CoG by `aft·sin θ − cog.y·(1 − cos θ)`
(aft = `length/2 + cog.z`) to keep the transom corner at the waterline — or
overshoot it and every probe goes dry, which is just as convincing. Prefer
the DYNAMIC bench for any attitude the hull reaches under its own forces:
apply the input and let it find its trim and its draft together. The static
one answers "what is the moment at this pose", which is only the question
when the pose is one the hull actually holds.
