---
title: For anything acting on an approach, HOW LONG it acts is a bigger lever than how hard — and the short firm version is the better game
date: 2026-09-10
scope: engine/game/flight.ts, engine/game/defs/tuning.ts
concepts: [flight, tuning, attitude, game-feel]
---

Sweeping `assist.right` against `assist.window` over a bench of staged
launches: at the SAME gain, stretching the window from 0.75 s to 1.15 s took
bad landings from 38% to 14% — while at the same window, five times the gain
took 38% to 11%. A fifth of the stiffness reaching half a second further out
buys the same landings.

They are not equivalent for the GAME, though, and the bench cannot say so.
The long soft version buys its result by owning most of the hang; the short
firm one leaves the rider the whole flight but its last three-quarters of a
second, and what he gets then reads as a catch rather than as a rail. Take
the short window and pay for it in gain.

Same shape applies to any approach-triggered term. Sweep the two together —
one alone reads as "this barely does anything" at exactly the setting where
the other would have made it work.
