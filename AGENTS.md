# Agent guidance for Sea Haven (game3)

This file is the canonical source of truth for AI coding agents working in this repo. `CLAUDE.md`, `.cursorrules`, `.windsurfrules`, `GEMINI.md`, and `.github/copilot-instructions.md` are symlinks to it (`tests/symlinks_test.ts` holds them).

**This file is the ROUTER, not the manual.** It says how work is done here, where things live at one level of detail, and which skill owns the rest. The procedures — every loop, every lab, every craft rule — live in `.agents/skills/`. Load the skill that owns the task's SUBJECT before starting; do not re-derive from this file what a skill already states.

This repository conforms to [`OSS_GAME_SPEC.md`](OSS_GAME_SPEC.md) — the committed copy IS the spec, self-contained, with no upstream document to fetch and no validator to call; it is a verbatim copy of the sibling rally game's, one spec for both games, and amending it is a reviewed PR that propagates the new mandate into the tree. When in doubt about layout, naming, or workflow conventions, the spec is the tie-breaker. Where the repo knowingly falls short of it, [`docs/spec-conformance.md`](docs/spec-conformance.md) is the ledger — one row per chapter, with the verdict, the evidence and what closing the gap would take; the `sync-game-spec` skill walks it.

**This repository is a VERTICAL SLICE.** The engine — the water, the wind, the craft, the course, the generator, the bot — is built and green. The app around it is the first playable cut: three coasts to build a shore on — the TAIGA, a cold sheltered skerry coast of granite, pine and grey-green brackish water; the MANGROVE, a warm flat coast of white sand, mangrove and palms on a clear turquoise sea with a groundswell in it; and the ARCTIC, a polar coast whose shore is a wall of glacier ice over black water at the freezing point, whose river is a crack in the ice, and whose winter sea is a SHEET OF ICE with an icebreaker's channel cut down the course (R37) — each with its own water, shore paint, cover, birds, sea life, skies, its own dating of the year and its GRADE — the cast its whole finished picture is graded with, cold and flat on the one, warm and split on the second, high-key and steel-blue on the third — chosen on the start card (a biome is a kind of coast, never a place: nothing in this tree names a country or a sea, and `tests/biome_test.ts` holds it to that) — one craft with its rider on the saddle, a ladder of six cameras, a full sky (the sun moving an hour a minute from the level's hour, in the level's season, at the coast's latitude — a night under the moon and the stars, the craft's lamp on the water — six weathers, a cloud deck), a HUD with a minimap of the course in it and a score over the nose for the air and the flips turned in it, keyboard and touch, the sound (the engine and the pump, the spray and the wind, the sea and the surf, every splash — all of it synthesized, nothing a file), and the shell around all of it — an attract card, a front door whose first tile is the CAMPAIGN — two shores of six pinned levels, ridden for points against a field that is never leaned on, each level a seed on a generator VERSION with a digest the suite holds it to — and whose next four are the four ways onto water, three of them riding those same twelve shores for the record book and the fourth picking a seed of its own, over a bot-ridden sea, a developer page behind a seven-second hold, a loading card over a run being stood up, a pause card that holds a run where it stands, and the REPLAY — every measured run written down as the controls that rode it, watched back from the finish plate or from the pause card, on a TV camera that cuts to the moments the run itself marked and runs them in slow motion. Around THAT, both platform shells are built: `tauri/`, the desktop app — a window in the platform's own webview over the site served from a private scheme, with a macOS menu bar whose every row presses a button the game already has — and `native/`, the store app — an Expo WebView over a copy of the site packed inside it, plus the phone's haptics under the game's own vibration table. Everything else is a placeholder file with a header comment saying what will live there, and this file says so wherever it routes to one. Do not describe a placeholder as a feature, and do not build into one without loading `engine-system` first.

## Build and test commands

```sh
npm install       # everything resolves from the public npm registry
make build        # typecheck + production build (pwa/dist/)
make test         # vitest over the engine (SHARD=i/N slices it; CI runs four)
make lint         # eslint + typecheck, zero warnings
make fmt          # prettier in place; fmt-check is what CI runs
make hooks        # install pre-commit + commit-msg hooks
make icons        # regenerate icons/favicon from the app mark
make previews     # the cards' pictures: the layouts (routes) and the coast banners (coasts)
make tauri-test   # the desktop shell's decision layer (Rust; needs no GUI libraries)
make native-typecheck  # the store shell's own tsc (its tree is installed on its own)
```

That is the everyday set. **NEITHER PLATFORM SHELL IS ON THE ROOT SUITE'S PATH** — `make test` and `make lint` stop at `tauri/`'s and `native/`'s edges, and each tree is checked by its own peers: `make tauri-test` / `tauri-lint` / `tauri-fmt` (`.github/workflows/desktop-tauri.yml` runs them on every push that touches the tree) and `make native-typecheck` (`native.yml` runs it before an EAS build, and eslint ignores `native/**` because a root `npm ci` never installs its plugins or its types). What the root suite does hold is what each shell RESTATES and cannot import: `tests/tauri_test.ts` reads the Rust as text, and `tests/shell_test.ts` / `tests/rumble_test.ts` read the store shell's three import-free modules directly — neither needs a toolchain. **The full list — every lab, what each one prints — is the README's Usage table, and the `Makefile` is the authority.** The table below says which of them a given change OWES.

**Scope the linter, never the typechecker, and leave the suite to CI.** `npx eslint <changed files>` is seconds where the whole repo is tens; `npx tsc --noEmit` must stay whole-program, because it checks a PROGRAM (naming files makes it ignore `tsconfig.json`) and because a changed signature breaks its CALLERS — the files you did not touch.

**`make fmt` is the Make target to run; `make test` is CI's.** The suite compiles whole levels, builds their sea and rides a craft through them at 120 Hz, and CI fans it out four ways on every push — so locally, run the FILES that cover the change (`npx vitest run tests/<topic>_test.ts`) and let the PR find the rest. The Make target is still the definition of green.

`waves`, `ride`, `crafts`, `level`, `analyze` and `audition` are pure Node — no build, no browser, seconds (the audition's `--meter` is the one browser-driven half: it drives the page it wrote in Chromium and prints every level). `screenshots`, `profile`, `sky`, `flora` and `birds` are browser-driven. The first two drive the BUILT SITE, so **`make build` first, every time**: a stale dist photographs the last change rather than this one, and the picture that comes back is wrong in a way that reads as a bug in the code. `sky`, `flora`, `birds`, `wake` and `glyphs` build their own one-off bundle from a harness page and so need no `make build`. Every lab that builds or photographs a level takes `BIOME=mangrove` or `BIOME=arctic` (`level`, `analyze`, `sim`, `waves`, `screenshots`, `sky`, `flora`, `birds`): the taiga is the default, and a change to a shared table is judged on every coast. The arctic's winter is the ice (R37): `make level SEED=n BIOME=arctic ARGS=--season=winter` draws the sheet and the channel, and `make screenshots BIOME=arctic ARGS=--season=winter` rides it. In Claude web sessions Chromium is preinstalled — prefix the browser-driven ones with `CHROMIUM_PATH=/opt/pw-browsers/chromium`.

Five of these are worth knowing about even when they are not your subject:

- **`make level SEED=38`** reasons about ONE level without riding it — every gate numbered with its offshore distance and the depth under it, every ramp, every rock, the wind arrow — in a couple of seconds. A claim about "the second air gate on seed 38" is a claim about a row there.
- **`make surf SEED=38`** is the sea as a RIDER meets it rather than as it was built: waves counted by zero-up-crossing, so the number that comes back is how far apart the crests actually stand and how often one meets the bow — the two things a tangle of components cannot be read for. Its last panel draws the three things that put white on a sea apart from each other, which is the only way to tell surf from spume.
- **`make waves SEED=38`** is the sea with nothing riding it: a transect from the shore out, Hs against offshore distance, the spectrum. A wave-model change is judged by the sea it makes, and a screenshot shows one wave.
- **`make sim`** is CI's `simulate` job and exits non-zero when a craft finishes NO seed. Its digests are where a determinism regression shows first; `docs/simulation.md` says what every column means.
- **`make flora`** is every species on the shore side by side, each drawn at both ends of its own height band over a metre rule. Same reason as the sky's sheet: a screenshot of a RUN shows whichever species that stretch of coast happened to grow, at whatever range the craft happened to be, against a wood of everything else — so a reed that is too pale comes back looking like a shore that is fine.
- **`make wake`** is the trail from STRAIGHT ABOVE, one column a moment along a scripted run, so the sheet reads left to right as the wake being laid. It exists because every camera in the game is a chase camera: the trail is only ever seen end-on, down its own length, foreshortened to a stripe — while every reference photograph of a wake is from overhead. A V that stops opening too early, a road twice the width it should be and a mark that is missing from the map rather than merely invisible in it all look the same from behind, and each of them shipped that way.
- **`make sky`** is every weather against every three hours of the clock, day and night, on ONE coast in ONE season (`ARGS="--season=autumn"` for the black nights), as a single labelled sheet. It exists because a seed is dealt one sky (R19) in one season at one hour (R13), so a screenshot of a RUN can only ever say whether that one sky is wrong — and the sky here is a LADDER, which is judged side by side or not at all.

## How work is done here

Rules that apply to every task, before any subject skill has a say. They are restated here from the skills that own them because a session that gets them wrong gets them wrong from its first tool call:

- **LOOK AT THE SIBLING REPO BEFORE BUILDING ANYTHING THAT IS NOT THE WATER.** [`niclaslindstedt/game2`](https://github.com/niclaslindstedt/game2) is the rally game this repo shares [`OSS_GAME_SPEC.md`](OSS_GAME_SPEC.md) with — same spec, same layering, same conventions, same commit and skill machinery — and it is **further along in nearly everything**. It has written skills where this repo only reserves the name (soundtrack, crashes, debug tools, level rating, store listing and store shots), and a deeper `scripts/` shelf: a track schematic, more per-subject preview labs, tape record-and-replay, store preflight and shot sweeps. **Read its answer first and adapt it; do not reinvent one.** It is public, so a session reaches it read-only with `git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/niclaslindstedt/game2` (this repo cannot push there, and nothing in this tree may import from it — what comes across is the DESIGN, retyped in our vocabulary: a car is a craft, a stage is a shore, tarmac is water).
  - **What is ours alone, and where the sibling has no answer worth taking:** the sea (`water-feel`), the hull's reply to it (`craft-physics`, `craft-tuning`), the coast and what swims off it (`mapgen-improvement`, `nature`), the rider on the saddle. A rally game's grip model is not a planing hull, and a road is not a wave field — porting a shape from there into any of those is how this game stops being about water.
  - **What comes across nearly unchanged:** tooling and labs, the menu and shell furniture, the maintenance and release plumbing, store and platform work, and the procedure half of any skill. Adapting one of the sibling's skills is a real port — its subject must exist HERE first (this file's placeholder table is the list of subjects that do not), and it lands with its name and routing added to this file, `.agents/skills/README.md` and the `maintenance` registry, which `tests/skills_test.ts` holds.

- **Lint, typecheck and format ONCE, at the gate — not after every edit.** `make fmt` and `make lint` are the commit's gate (the `commit` skill owns the split). Re-running them between one edit and the next re-checks code nobody touched and tells you nothing; batch the whole coherent change, then check it. Mid-loop, if a specific answer is genuinely needed, check only the files you touched (`npx eslint <paths>`, `npx tsc --noEmit`) — never a whole-repo pass, and never `prettier`, whose every finding `make fmt` fixes at the end for free.
- **TEST WHAT YOU WROTE; THE PR TESTS THE REST.** Run the suites that cover the change and the ones it plausibly reaches, by file, and push — a red PR is a normal state and a follow-up commit costs nothing. Be honest about reach: a change to `TUNING`, `hull.ts`, `water.ts` or the generator reaches tests three directories away (a hull retune moves `craft_test`, `flight_test`, `simulation_test` and `determinism_test`'s digests at once; a rules change moves `mapgen_test`, `mapgen_population_test`, `analysis_test` and `docs_rules_test`), so name the topics generously for those; and a red PR is work NOW, not something to leave sitting.
- **THIS REPOSITORY IS PUBLIC — no personal details go in it.** Team ids, account names, tokens, keys, device ids, e-mail addresses, absolute paths under a home directory: none of them are committed, not even the ones that are identifiers rather than secrets, and not as a "default" a contributor can override. Everything of that kind is read from the ENVIRONMENT — a gitignored `.env` beside the tree that needs it (with the committed `.env.example` documenting the shape), and GitHub repository **secrets** for credentials or **variables** for identifiers on CI. The published app's own identifiers (`APP_NAME`, `SITE_URL`, `REPO_URL`) are the deliberate exception — they are the product's public name, and they live in `pwa/src/identity.ts`.
- **NAME NO REAL PRODUCT — not a game, not a machine.** Nothing in this tree names another game, a games console, a watercraft manufacturer or one of its models, or any other real brand: not in prose, not in a doc, not in a code comment, not in a skill or one of its lessons, and least of all in anything the player reads. **The feel reference is the 90s jetski racers** — the arcade generation this game is measured against, named as a GENRE and never as a title. Where a real machine's numbers are the honest source for a proportion or a tuning value, keep the NUMBER and the band it sits in and drop the badge ("a runabout is 1.14–1.24 m keel to bar-top", never a make and model). The generic words for what is ridden — jet ski, personal watercraft, runabout, stand-up — are descriptions, not brands, and stay; so does our own vocabulary (the craft, the hull, the skiff, the marlin, the otter, the dart) and the sibling repo `game2`, which is ours. A cited scientific source is not a brand either: Gerstner, JONSWAP, Pierson–Moskowitz, Savitsky and Michell are models and the people behind them, and they are named on purpose.
- **Every work session ends by committing its work with the `commit` skill.** Once the requested change and its gates are complete, load and follow that skill to make a conventional commit; when working in a worktree, follow its required sync step afterward.

## Architecture summary

Three layers, one direction of dependency (details: [docs/architecture.md](docs/architecture.md); enforced by `tests/imports_test.ts`):

- **`engine/`** — the whole game as a framework-free, renderer-free TypeScript module that imports NOTHING but itself. Fixed 120 Hz `step(state, input)` (`TUNING.physicsHz`; the bot decides on every step too), deterministic per seed (no `Math.random` at runtime — everything draws from the seeded RNG in state, `state.rng`). Contains the sea and the wind (`game/water.ts`, `wind.ts` — pure functions of `(x, z, t)` plus one seeded gust process, plus the WASH the hulls lay in it, `wash.ts`, a function of the ride), the craft (`game/craft.ts` summing `hull.ts`, `hydro.ts`, `propulsion.ts`, `flight.ts`, `assist.ts`, `collision.ts`), the course (`game/course.ts`), the level generator (`mapgen/`), the bot rider + headless simulator (`sim/`), the generator's scoreboard (`analysis/` — dev-time only, but it IS the generator's accept gate), the §19.4 output module (`output.ts`), and data-authored content (`game/defs/`). `engine/index.ts` is the one public surface; `@engine` is how every host spells it.
- **`pwa/`** — the browser shell: Preact app, three.js renderer (reads `GameState`, never steps physics — and displaces its water mesh by calling the engine's own `surfaceAt`, so what is drawn is what is simulated), input, HUD, the audio (`game/audio/` — every sound synthesized from authored parameters over one WebAudio instrument in `lib/synth.ts`; nothing is a file), PWA plumbing (hand-rolled service worker via `pwa-plugin.ts` + the update watch in `lib/pwa-update.ts`).
- **`tests/` + `scripts/`** — root-level vitest suites over the engine, and Node tooling (the sim CLI, the level map, the waves and ride labs, screenshots, icons, SEO checks, release plumbing; every tool parses its flags through `scripts/lib/cli.mjs`, which gives it `--help` and a non-zero exit on an unknown flag).

Beside them, OUTSIDE the npm workspace and outside the root suite's path, the two shells that wrap the built site, both BUILT: **`tauri/`** (the desktop app) is two Rust crates, `shell/` for every decision and `src-tauri/` for every effect, with its own `make tauri*` targets, its own workflow and a packaging matrix in `release.yml`; **`native/`** (the App Store / Play Store app) is an Expo/React Native WebView over a copy of the site packed inside it, with its own `make native-*` targets and a dispatch-only EAS workflow. **Nothing in `engine/` may learn either exists, and the ONE line of `pwa/` that does is `pwa/src/shell-host.ts`** (the frozen `__SH_SHELL__` global and the `sh-` events around it). A feature a shell needs is a feature the website needs first, and a menu row may only press a button the game already has.

The root suite may import from a shell in exactly one place: the pure, import-free seam modules named in `tests/imports_test.ts`'s `SHELL_SEAM` (`native/src/injected.ts`, `navigation.ts`, `rumble.ts`), which is how the names a shell restates and cannot import are held to the page's. A seam module that grows an import fails that test. The desktop shell's Rust is held the other way, as TEXT (`tests/tauri_test.ts`), because a root suite may not carry a toolchain.

**Hard rules:** the engine never imports three.js, Preact, `node:` or anything from `pwa/` or `scripts/` (framework-free, and the test says so); the renderer never mutates `GameState`; engine randomness only via `state.rng` (`tests/determinism_test.ts` + `tests/imports_test.ts` enforce it — and the wind draws its gusts off `state.rng` every step whether or not anything feels them, so a run replays the same stream); source files stay under 1000 lines (`tests/file_size_test.ts`, §20.5's marker for the honest exceptions); the engine prints only through `engine/output.ts`. **The game ships no asset files** — every hull, every wave and every shore is code.

### The role map, and what is generated

The spec (§23) names roles, not directories; this is the mapping, and the arrows are rules a review may refuse a change against. `engine/` is the **simulation core** (§23.1) with `engine/index.ts` as its one public entry surface; `pwa/` is the **presentation shell** (§23.2); `tauri/` and `native/` are **platform shells** (§23.3 — both built); `scripts/` is **tooling** (§23.6) and may import anything while nothing imports it. There is no session service — the game is single-player (§34 does not apply). The core imports nothing from a shell or a script; a shell imports the core, never another shell; the suite reaches the core through `@engine` like a host does.

**Content (§23.5) is the deliberate deviation**: this game's levels are GENERATED from a seed rather than authored, and its small fixed catalogs (`engine/game/defs/craft.ts`, `tuning.ts`; `mapgen/biomes.ts`, `mapgen/rules.ts`) are TypeScript consts rather than schema-validated data files. `docs/spec-conformance.md` carries the reasoning, mirrored from the sibling repo's, and what changing it would cost — do not start converting a catalog to data on the strength of §24 alone.

What IS generated is generated, and **a generated artifact is never hand-edited**:

### Stated once — never restate these

Each of these is the one place an answer is written down. Anything that needs it ASKS; a second copy is a bug the day one of them moves.

- **WHAT A BUOY'S LAMP IS DOING** — `buoyLightAt(light, t)` in `engine/game/buoy.ts`: the flash CHARACTER the generator drew (`Fl(3) 8s` — a group of flashes and how often it comes round), as a pure function of the level's own clock, exactly as the sea life's pose is. Nothing about a lamp is stepped, stored or replayed; `pwa/src/game/buoys.ts` reads this once per buoy per frame for the lens and the bloom, and hands the same number to the water so the pool on the sea pulses with it. WHICH can is burning is that file's own rule and not the engine's (`buoyLamp` beside `markOf`, the gate marks' `markLamp` for a can): only the checkpoint the run is riding at — and the one a charged rider is being sent back to — and only after dark.
- **WHICH KIND OF TRACK a level is** — `Level.track` (`coast` or `circuit`), and `Course.laps` / `Course.lapGates` beside it. A lapped course PUBLISHES THE WHOLE RIDE — the lap's gates repeated, with one more crossing of the start line for the finish — so `stepCourse` takes the list in order and never learns a lap exists; `lapGates` is how the renderer draws each buoy once and the HUD counts laps. A coast course is `laps: 1` with `lapGates` the whole gate count, so nothing has to branch to read one.
- **What a craft CAN do** — `engine/game/limits.ts` (`maxRpm`, `maxNozzle`, `MAX_LEAN`, `jetCeiling`, `airPitchTorque`, `topSpeedOf`, `runUpTo`), read by the physics AND `sim/bot.ts`. Never restate a ceiling. `runUpTo(spec, share)` is the odd one out — how much WATER a hull needs to reach a share of its top speed, a closed form off the spec's own `accel0to50`, asked at BUILD time by R35 and never by the physics.
- **WHICH END OF A RIVAL YOU MET** — `clipHulls(a, b)` in `engine/game/hull-contact.ts`, and the `hullShell` under it. A hull is an oriented shell — keel to deck, transom to stem, its plan taper and its keel's rocker read off the hull's OWN tables (`TUNING.hull.stationTaper`, `.stationRise`) so the outside another hull meets cannot drift from the bottom the water reads — and a contact is resolved out through whichever of its six faces is the shallowest way back out, the ramp wedge's rule generalised. Everything the contact does follows from WHICH face that is and at what HEIGHT, and nothing about it is special-cased: a flank stops a rider across her and its manifold squares the two of them up; a transom met with the bow low pushes under her centre of gravity and lifts her nose, met with the bow high pushes above it and buries it, and met higher still rides over onto her deck; two raked bows shoulder each other outward. The solver is sequential-impulse with the rotational term in the effective mass (without it a contact hands out free rotation, which is the pirouette), and the only arcade in it is `RACE.bump`'s three per-axis gains, the ceiling on what one step of one meeting is worth, and the couple that lines two rafted hulls up. `rivals.ts` decides which PAIRS are asked and whose contact earns a `bump`; it decides nothing about what a contact does.
- **EVERY RAMP ON A LEVEL** — `rampsOf(level)` in `engine/game/collision.ts`: a deck comes from two places (an air gate's, R8; the trick field's, R35 — `Level.ramps`) and nothing outside that function should have to know it. Cached per level, because it is asked once a physics step.
- **WHAT THE RIDER IS AIMING AT** — `aimPoint(state)` in `engine/game/course.ts`: the next GATE in a run that counts the course, the next LIP ahead of the hull in one that does not. The guide line under the water reads it and no surface asks the question a second way.
- **HOW FAR APART A TRICKS RUN'S RAMPS STAND** — `trickStride(pace)` in `engine/mapgen/pace.ts` (R35): the longest run-up any hull in the catalog needs to reach `TRICK_SHARE` of its top speed from rest, plus the deck and R7's landing. Calibrated on the WORST craft rather than per craft, so one seed is one field and a tricks score stays comparable across the roster. The catalog's answer is not intuition's: the DART is the slowest craft and needs the LEAST road.
- **What the speedo reads** — `CraftState.speed`: `|v|`, vertical included, written once at the end of `stepCraft`. The HUD, the bot and the sim all read it and none restates it.
- **Where an animal is** — `faunaPose(pod, i, t, out, waterY)` in `engine/game/fauna.ts`: the sea life's `surfaceAt`, a pure function of the pod's loop, the clock and the water over the pod (every depth in it is measured down from `waterY` — `surfaceAt`'s height at the pod, sampled once per pod per frame, not from the plane y = 0). The rise through the surface, how far out of it the animal comes (`rise`) and a bull's breach (`isMale`, `breach`) are all here. Nothing about the fauna is stepped, stored per frame or replayed, and `pwa/src/game/fauna.ts` reads this and nothing else.
- **WHETHER THE SEA IS ICE** — `frozen(level)` in `engine/game/ice.ts`, and `iceTopAt` beside it (R37). A freezing coast's level carries the ice FIELD (`Level.ice`, metres inside the sheet, laid off the finished course by `mapgen/sea-ice.ts` from nothing the seeded stream draws); whether the sheet is THERE is the run's season, read here and nowhere else — a run re-seasoned by `createGame` is the same shore with the ice on it or off it. The hull meets the sheet through `bedAt` (it is ground: a rider who leaves the channel rides up the brash and grounds), the sea meets it in `createSea` (a lead grows chop and no swell, `TUNING.sea.ice`), the renderer draws it from the same field (`pwa/src/game/sea-ice.ts`) and leaves the pods under it undrawn, and the level map paints it. Nothing else decides where the ice is.
- **WHAT LIES PAST THE EDGE OF THE LEVEL** — `engine/game/ocean.ts`. A level is a grid a couple of kilometres across and the sea has no far side: `stormRamp` is the ONE ramp saying how much of the open ocean's storm a point is in, and the sea's OPEN band (`water.ts`), the bed a hull grounds on (`bedAt`), the mean wind (`oceanWind`) and the bounds that let a rider out (`collision.ts`'s `boundsPush`) all read it. Nothing out there is baked — a grid reaching that far is a level's build time spent on water nobody rides — so everything past the rim is analytic, and this is where it is stated.
- **WHAT SEA A POINT IS DEALT** — `seaShares(sea, x, z)` over the FOUR bands: the sea this coast's wind grew, the chop on water that sea cannot reach, the GROUNDSWELL that came from somebody else's weather (quoted, not grown — and gated off at zero wind, which in this engine means a flat calm), and the storm past the rim. Nothing else decides how much of which sea stands anywhere.
- **The wave surface** — `surfaceAt(sea, level, x, z, t)` in `engine/game/water.ts`: the height, the normal and the water's velocity — the waves' orbital motion AND the river's current (R27), because "how fast is the water going here" is one question. The hull probes call it at 120 Hz and the renderer's `water-mesh.ts` calls the SAME function to displace its vertices. There is no second wave function anywhere.
- **WHAT THE CRAFT DID TO THE WATER** — `washAt` in `engine/game/wash.ts`, summed into `surfaceAt` for every sample that carries the whole field: the waves a hull LEFT, as against the waves the sea brought. Each rider's trail (`GameState.wash`) is a ring buffer of sources laid by his own step — one per metre of passage, the bob on a cadence, a splash at every `land` and `dive` — and every trail stands on the one sea (`SeaState.washes`), so a rival's wake is on the player's water and the player's on his. One wavelength (the hump speed's, `TUNING.wash.hump`), one decay, one rule for what strikes a source; the V, the squat and the rings are all the sum. A hull feels its OWN wash only outside its footprint and only once it is two seconds old, which is what lets it cross its trail in a turn without riding up on the forward arc of the ring it laid a hull length ago. Nothing draws from `state.rng`; `placeRun` clears it. The renderer's wake map (`pwa/src/game/wake.ts`) restates NONE of it — the map carries the white, the churn, and only the relief that stands inside the hull's own footprint (the transom's hollow and mound, a landing's crater); the arms of the V, a splash's ring and a bob's ring are this and this alone. `make wash` is the picture, `tests/wash_test.ts` the claims.
- **HOW MUCH OF A PIECE OF SEA HAS GONE OVER** — `breakingParts` in `pwa/src/game/water-break.ts`: the three things that put white on water, kept apart because they are not the same thing and do not live in the same place. The SURF is the bed tripping the wave, read off the sea's height over the most the depth can hold (`depthLoad`, against the engine's own `TUNING.sea.breakingHs` clip) — a band off every shore, whatever the wind is doing. The CREST is the top of a steep wave spilling in deep water. The CAPS are the wind blowing the tops off, and the wind band is the only thing that tells a coast from a gale. Three-free, so `water-mesh.ts` (per vertex, into the foam field), `make surf` and `tests/water_break_test.ts` all read ONE statement of it.
- **WHAT IS AT THE FAR EDGE OF THAT OCEAN** — `engine/game/tornado.ts`. The storm stops building at `sea.open.reach`, and a rider who keeps going is on a ceiling sea no level was built for; `tornadoEdge(level.pace)` (that reach plus a minute of riding at the roster's fastest AT THIS RUN'S CLASS — R32 made the class per-run, and the edge, the band and the blow all read it, while the climb and the column are quoted against the SEA, which `createSea` deals off the catalog and so does not move per run) is where something else starts instead. `tornadoNetPlan` is that edge as a SHAPE — the box and the distance out — stated once so the lattice `pwa/src/game/edge-net.ts` draws there stands exactly where `tornadoAt` bites, corners included (the locus is a ROUNDED rectangle, because `oceanOffset` reads a corner as a hypotenuse). `tornadoInflow` is the wind — toward `level.start`, from whichever rim he left by, spiralled cyclonically — and it reaches the hull through `windAt` like any other wind, so nothing learned a new force to feel it. `tornadoLift` is the UPDRAFT, which cannot go through the same term because `cdA` is the hull's drag area nose-on and a hull going up is not going forwards: it is the plan area as a flat plate, scaled by `airShare`, which is what makes the hazard what it is — shoved about on the water, and taken the moment a wave throws him clear. HOW MUCH OF IT A RIDER GETS IS READ OFF HOW HIGH HE IS: `tornadoHeightGain` scales the COLUMN — not the inflow — from `tornado.deck` at the water to all of it at `reachUp`, against MEAN sea level so a hull carried up on a crest counts as high, which is what makes meeting the net down in a trough a contact that turns him back and meeting it off a crest a throw. `tornadoTilt` is the inflow turning UP in the first couple of metres, on a floating hull's deck as much as on one in the air, and it is the only lift there is down there. Bounded twice, and both were needed: the sign on the relative speed bounds the CLIMB, and `tornadoColumn`'s top bounds the HEIGHT (without one the roster went to 150 m for seventeen seconds). It resets nothing, ends nothing and stands no wall; `tests/tornado_test.ts` holds all of that and holds the zone clear of every gate of every seed.
- **HOW BIG THE SEA IS PAST THE RIM** — `jumpableHs(speed)` and `STORM_CEILING` (`engine/game/ocean.ts`): the biggest sea the roster's FASTEST craft can still fly over the rim of and down to the floor of, which is a closed form QUADRATIC in the top speed (a wave's width grows with its height, a flight's reach does not, so the two cross once). Nothing anywhere quotes an ocean height: ride a faster CLASS and the ocean grows with the square of it. Each level then DEALS its own storm over the top `open.vary` of that ceiling, so the biggest is rare — one seed in ten. `createSea` lays a band per rung of `open.rungs` so each height keeps its own wavelength, and `stormSeaAt` is what the renderer judges a crest against out there.
- **WHAT A SPEED CLASS DOES TO A CRAFT** — `craftAtClass(spec, class)` in `engine/game/defs/craft.ts`: the ONE place a class is applied, and it applies it by returning a SPEC — a taller impeller, the engine grown to swing it (the pump's load goes as pitch³, so an engine that did not grow would bog), and the `topSpeed`, `powerKw` and `accel0to50` the result actually has. `topSpeedOf`, `jetCeiling`, the pump, the bot, the HUD's dial and the craft card's spec sheet all read that spec and none of them knows a class exists. `CLASS_BAND` is the ladder a build offers; the CATALOG's numbers stay the hull's own AT CLASS 1, because what separates the four must not move when a class does.
- **WHICH QUARTER THE WIND BLOWS FROM** — `windQuarter(level, from)` / `windFromQuarter(level, quarter)` in `engine/game/wind.ts`, against `Level.seaHeading`: the wind's bearing quoted off DEAD ONSHORE rather than off the compass, because a level's own bearing is whatever the seed drew and "from 2.4 rad" says nothing about whether the sea is being driven at the beach or blown flat off it. Zero is straight in off the open water — the middle of the band R12 deals from — a right angle either way is along the shore, half a turn is off the land behind. It is not dressing: the quarter is what the fetch is measured along (`fetch.ts`), so a wind turned off the sea grows no waves however hard it blows, which is why `createGame`'s `windQuarter` option is FREE's alone. `Level.seaHeading` is published for this one purpose; the generator has always had it (`Route.seaHeading`).
- **What the wind crossed to get here** — `createShelter(level, wind)` in `engine/game/fetch.ts`: the effective fetch, the exposure to the open sea and the shelter over the plan, measured ONCE per run and read by both the sea (R28's two bands) and the wind. Nothing else decides whether a piece of water is the ocean's or a river's.
- **What the shore is made of** — `Level.materialAt(x, z)`; the sea's `surfaceAt` is the WAVE surface, which is why the level's classifier is not called that.
- **The ramp's anchor** — `rampSurface` in `engine/mapgen/course.ts`: `(x, z)` is the HINGE at the waterline, `length` is the plan footprint, the lip stands `length · tan(angle)` up. The collision engine's `rampDeckY` is the same line, and the search, the analysis and the tests all place a ring off it (`ringPlacement`).
- **Where a reset stands the craft** — `resetPose` in `engine/game/course.ts`; `standCraft` is how anything puts a craft down afloat at its rest draft (`restY` in `hull.ts` — Archimedes, bisected).
- **The heading to the next gate** — `bearingToNext` in `engine/game/course.ts`, for the HUD's arrow and the bot alike.
- **How far down the course a run has got** — `gatesReached` in `engine/game/course.ts`: gates taken plus gates charged for. The HUD's `n / N` counter and the minimap's gauge are the same reading in two forms, and neither restates the sum.
- **The sign conventions** — heading 0 = +z, clockwise from above; pitch NOSE-UP positive; roll RIGHT-SIDE-DOWN positive; body angular rates right-handed. `engine/lib/quat.ts`'s `fromEuler`/`toEuler` own the flip between the rider's reading and the algebra's. The one place the SCREEN's axes (thumb toward you, drag down for throttle) are turned into the engine's signs is `pwa/src/game/input-model.ts`, DOM-free so the tests can read it; `input.ts` only feeds it events.
- **What sky a level is under** — `Level.weather` (R19), `Level.season` and the hour the run has reached (`sunHourAt(level, state.t)` — an hour of sun a minute of riding, stated in `engine/game/clock.ts`), with `skyCover(wind.speed)` the one measure of how heavy that sky is. `pwa/src/game/sky.ts`'s `skyAt` turns them into a `Preset`, every frame, and everything that answers to the sky — the two lights, the fog, the dome, the clouds, the stars, what the water reflects, the craft's lamp switch (`Preset.lamps`) — reads that ONE preset. The HUD's own night dressing (`snapshot.ts`'s `dark`, `--hud-dark` in `styles.css`) is the same switch read through `lampsAt` rather than a threshold of its own, so the cluster and the beam can never disagree. Nothing anywhere else decides how dark it is.
- **What the water reflects** — `skyAlong(ray, origin)` in `pwa/src/game/sky-glsl.ts`: the whole sky in one GLSL function, gradient, sun and cloud sheets, and the dome, the water's mirror AND the craft's gel coat (`craft-surface.ts`) are all painted with it off ONE shared uniform bundle (`createSkyUniforms`, written by `environment.ts`); `mirrorBuild` is the one rough-mirror build the water and the craft are compiled with. The sea reflects the sky over it by construction rather than by two files agreeing; only what each was COMPILED for differs (`SkyBuild` — the water carries no sun and reads the sheets blurred, because a rough mirror smears a sharp sky into streaks that read as foam). `seaMirror(preset)` is the same question asked for ONE grazing angle, the horizon ring's. `skyToneAt` in `sky.ts` is the gradient as TypeScript, which is what `sky_test.ts` holds the model to.
- **WHAT A COAST IS GRADED** — `gradeOf(level.biome)` in `pwa/src/game/colour-grade.ts`: seven dials over the WHOLE finished frame — the contrast and the lift in a perceptual coordinate, the saturation, one cast, and a SPLIT pulling the darks and the lights toward two different colours. It is the grade a television series is finished with rather than the light it was shot under: the cold coast comes back flat, drained and blue in its shadows, the warm one punchy with gold on what the sun is on and teal on everything else. Every colour in a row is normalised by its own luminance, so a row states a HUE and can never state an exposure. `gradeTone` is the model in TypeScript (`tests/colour_grade_test.ts` holds both looks to it) and `grade-pass.ts`'s fragment shader is the same arithmetic in GLSL. It is applied ONCE, to the composited picture: the whole frame is drawn into that pass's multisampled target instead of onto the canvas (which therefore asks for no samples of its own — how many the target takes is `pictureSamples` in `settings-video.ts`, read off the DEVICE's pixel ratio rather than off a video row, because the buffer is allocated with the target and no row can re-make it mid-run), and one screen-filling pair of triangles grades it and writes the canvas. Grading inside the materials instead — three's `CustomToneMapping` hook will inject a function into every one of them for no pass at all — grades every LAYER of a transparent pixel separately before they are blended, and the sea, the spray and the rain are all transparent; it also has nowhere to put a uniform, so a per-coast grade would mean recompiling every material in the scene. Nothing else anywhere tints the picture.
- **WHAT A COAST'S WATER IS MADE OF** — `waterOpticsOf(level.biome)` in `pwa/src/game/water-optics.ts`: the three tones and the depths they run over, the surface's window, the flat unlit tone the bottom fades into, and `clarity` — how far the eye gets into that water. `clarity` is the ONE depth scale the whole see-through model is written against: the window reaches its deep stop over it (`water-mesh.ts`), the sea bed fades into the bed tone over it (`terrain.ts`), and an animal hazes toward the water over it (`fauna.ts`). Nothing restates a water colour or a depth of its own, and the surface's alpha is never the thing that hides the bottom — it is one number for a patch of sea and cannot tell a fish at two metres from a bed at twenty.
- **WHAT SIZE THE FOAM IS ON THE WATER** — `pwa/src/game/water-foam.ts`: the tile's world footprint across the wind and downwind, the window a vertex's share slides over it, and the second, incommensurate reading of the same tile that decides WHERE the foam is dense. The sea's lace and the wake road's are both cut there, in GLSL injected into `water-shader.ts`, so a foam scale is never stated twice; the tile itself is drawn on a torus in `fx-textures.ts` (`tiledValueNoise`), so it meets itself at its own edges.
- **HOW LONG FOAM LIVES** — `pwa/src/game/foam-field.ts`. What the water mesh reads off the surface is what is BREAKING at this instant, and a world point is at the top of a wavelet for about a tenth of a second; foam is what the crest LEFT, standing in the water while the wave rolls on and dying over seconds. So the mesh sows the breaking into this field — anchored to the world, wrapping round the craft, decaying at `FOAM_LIFE` — and the share a vertex carries is the louder of what it sowed and what it read. Nothing else anywhere keeps a foam lifetime: the wake's road has its own map (`wake.ts`) and takes the louder of the two in the shader.
- **How far a rider can see INTO the water** — `WaterMesh.seeThrough()` in `pwa/src/game/water-mesh.ts`: inside that radius the near grid is semi-transparent and the horizon ring has its hole; outside it the far water is opaque. Anything drawn under the surface is drawn only inside it, and asks the mesh rather than recomputing it — the radius moves with the WATER row and is 0 when the rider has closed the window, so one number carries both the reach and the setting.
- **WHAT A FRAME COST** — `FrameCost` in `pwa/src/game/benchmark-report.ts`, and `SceneShare` beside it. Stated THERE rather than in `renderer.ts` for one reason: the report, the history and the score sheet are DOM-free and the root suite reads them, and a type imported from the renderer would drag three.js into a suite that runs on plain Node. `renderer.ts` imports both and re-exports them, so there is still one name for one thing; the HUD's FRAME COST row and the benchmark's report read the same object.
- **WHAT A RUN WAS RIDDEN ON, TO THE FIGURE** — `snapInput` in `pwa/src/game/ghost.ts`, applied by `input-model.ts`'s `sampleInput` on the way out. Every axis the player produces is snapped onto the tape's own grid (1/127 of full lock and lean, 1/255 of a lever) at the ONE place the player's input is made, so the figure the engine is ridden on IS the figure a recording writes down and a GHOST is the run again rather than nearly it. Record anything the engine did not receive and the replay walks off the line a gate later. The grid is finer than a thumb or a key ramp can resolve, so nothing about the riding moved — and the bot never passes through it, so no digest did either.
- **WHAT A REPLAY CUTS TO, AND HOW SLOWLY IT RUNS** — `pwa/src/game/replay-shots.ts`. A live camera cannot cut BEFORE a jump, because it has no way of knowing one is coming; a replay can, because the run wrote the moment down as it happened. The collector watches `state.events` on the player's own run and files four kinds — a flight that stayed up, what was turned in it, another hull met at speed, and it going wrong — BACK-DATED to the step the hull left the water, since what makes a flight worth watching is only known at the landing. `planShots` is the running order: ranked by weight and then spaced, which is what takes the BIG one out of a crowded pair rather than the first one. `directAt(plan, step)` is the whole edit as a pure function of how far into the recording the picture has got, and it answers both questions at once — which moment holds the frame, and the TIME RATE, which is slow motion and is a replay's alone because nobody is steering. Slow motion is FEWER STEPS PER FRAME and nothing else: the engine's step never changes size, so a third-speed backflip is the same physics at a third the rate, every wave and every sound with it. `camera-tv.ts` decides where the lens stands and restates none of this.
- **WHAT THE GAME REMEMBERS** — `pwa/src/game/settings.ts`, and `mergeSettings` is the one place a stored blob is turned into settings this build offers. No surface keeps a preference of its own beside it, and the ONE other thing the game keeps between visits is the SCREENSHOT ROLL — which is not a setting and not in localStorage: a picture is hundreds of kilobytes of PNG, so `pwa/src/lib/shot-store.ts` is an IndexedDB store (the one browser API that takes a Blob as a Blob), with `shot-roll.ts` beside it holding the policy — newest first, forty kept, oldest off — storage-free so the tests read it. It is never load-bearing: a browser with no IndexedDB keeps the roll in memory for the life of the tab, and a picture is never lost over a store that would not open.
- **WHAT THE PLAYER PRESSES** — `pwa/src/game/settings-input.ts`: every action a key can be bound to, whether it is HELD or taken on the press (`isHeldAction`, off a table typed `Record<HeldAction, true>` so a seventh held key does not compile until it is named), the shipped layout and why each key is where it is, and how a code reads on the cap. `input.ts` builds its code → actions index from it and knows no particular key; `menu-keys.tsx` is the page that changes one; `settings.ts` only stores the answer. The HELD set is `keyof KeysHeld` — never restated.
- **WHAT A PICTURE ROW COSTS** — `pwa/src/game/settings-video.ts`: the dictionary between a row of OPTIONS ▸ VIDEO and the numbers the renderer needs (the water grid's three dimensions, the ripple reach, the anisotropy, the pixel share, the spray budget, the flora share, the draw radii and the haze over them). DOM-free, so `tests/video_test.ts` reads the whole ladder; `renderer.setVideo` is the one place a row becomes a draw call.
- **HOW MUCH WORLD IS DRAWN** — `DISTANCE_LOOK` in `settings-video.ts`, applied by `cullByDistance` (`pwa/src/game/draw-distance.ts`) to the shore's chunks and the cover's stands, and by `Environment.setHaze` to the fog. The row's promise is that the two halves are ONE number: every stop pulls the fog in until it has closed before its own radii, so nothing it stops drawing was visible. `tests/video_test.ts` holds the table to that against the clearest sky the generator can deal — never restate a radius without the haze that hides it.
- **WHICH SURFACE IS UP** — `Shell` in `pwa/src/game/shell.ts` (`splash | menu | loading | pause | run | bench`), and ONE engine state carries through all six: the shell only decides who rides it (`playerRides` — `botInput` under a card, the input manager under a run), whether the engine steps at all (`simulates`), whether the app's own loop DRAWS (`appDraws`), whether the HUD is over it (`hudOver`) and where the pause card may be reached from (`canPause`). DOM-free, so `tests/menu_system_test.ts` holds all five; `App.tsx` is the one module that decides WHEN one surface gives way to the next. **THE SEA NEVER STOPS BEHIND A CARD — except the pause card**, which stands over the PLAYER's own run rather than the bot's, and is the one surface `simulates` says no to. **THE BENCH IS A SECOND, DIFFERENT NO**: the benchmark pumps its own frames as fast as the machine will draw them (`benchmark.ts`), so the app's loop neither steps nor draws while one is up — not because the water is held, but because somebody else is turning it.
- **WHERE THE EAR IS** — `LISTENERS` in `pwa/src/game/audio/listener.ts`, one row per rung of the camera ladder: what a seat does to the engine, the exhaust, the pump, the hull, the wind, the sea and the one-shots. The beds take the numbers they need as a MIX argument and the router applies `events` and `muffle`; nothing anywhere else branches on the camera to decide how loud something is. And **THE HULL'S SLAM** — `CraftState.slam`, N, written once by `craft.ts` off `hull.ts`'s wedge impact — is what the bed reads to raise a slap; nothing restates when a slam is a sound.
- **Where a bird is** — `birdPose(flock, i, t, out, activity, waterY, flushedAt)` in `pwa/src/game/bird-plan.ts`: the birds' own `faunaPose`, a pure function of the flock's routine (its roost, its loop, its cycle) and the engine's clock, with how much DAY there is read off the same sun the sky is lit by (`activityAt`) and the sea under a raft handed in the way the fauna's `waterY` is. The one memory is the caller's — when the craft last put a raft up — decided by `flushAt`, the rule stated once there: `birds.ts` keeps it beside the wake's to draw the raft going up, and `audio/bird-bed.ts` keeps its own to play the whirr of it. What CROSSES is `crossingAt(plan, k)`, a pure function of the crossing's index, so the sky on the first frame already has the skeins in it.
- **HOW FAR UNDER THE WATER THE HULL IS** — `submergedShare(bottomUnder, deckFill)` in `engine/game/submerged.ts`, off two readings `hull.ts` takes in its first pass: the LEAST-immersed bottom probe and the mean fill of the four deck probes. Their product is the one measure of the submerged regime, and everything that answers to being under reads it — the rider's own authority down there (the far end of `flight.ts`'s `airShare` crossing, which used to hand him nothing at all the moment the hull was wet), whether the engine is still running, and how much of the deck's sealed float has flooded (`floodedDeck`). It takes BOTH halves because either alone confuses two cases that must not be: the bottom alone calls a buried bow with a dry transom "under", and the deck alone says the same of a hull floating INVERTED, whose float the capsize depends on. A DEPTH cannot do it — `hull.submerged` is the deepest probe, so it reads a bow going in as a hull going under. Zero at rest on every craft, and engaged only in the last percentile of a head sea. THE SPELL is the same module's (`stepUnder`): `CraftState.under` is a latch on that share with a gap between going in and out, `underTime` its clock — the air's `airborne` / `airTime` on the other side of the surface, read by the HUD's clock and the score from `submerged.counts` on — and the FLOAT-UP is the rule it is played under: ten seconds on the gas, one off it, or a hull that comes out of a dive on its back, and the orientation is turned to upright and nose-up (`floatUpPose`, the capsize's righting on the other side of the surface) while the buoyancy brings it up. A dive never ends in a capsize, and the capsize clock does not run under the water. The sponsons' lever and the chines' bank-in both fade with the share, because both plane and a submerged hull rising heeled was rolled over by them.
- **WHAT A STROKE ON THE BARS IS WORTH** — `engine/game/strokes.ts`, which owns BOTH controls read off the SHAPE of their input rather than its value, because they are one mechanism read twice. THE PUMP is the bars hauled BACK and buys rotation nose-over-tail; THE WHIP is the bars thrown OVER and buys rotation about the hull's own length — the side spin. A stroke is ALL OR NOTHING and lives at the TOP of its axis: nothing at all below the gate (`flight.pumpGate` 0.8, `flight.whipGate` 0.85), the whole impulse on every fresh crossing of it, spent out of that flight's budget (`CraftState.pumped` / `.whipped`, bounded by `pumpCeiling` / `whipCeiling` times `riderAuthority`). ONLY MAXING AN AXIS IS A TRICK — that is what leaves a rider free to trim a head sea and hold a line without buying rotation, and it is why the app's lean keys ramp fast (a key reaches the top of an axis only by being held there) while its steer keys do not. And A TRICK CANNOT BE OPENED ON THE WAY DOWN: the FIRST stroke of a flight needs `vy > 0` at the moment it is asked for, and once one has landed the flight is latched a trick (`CraftState.tricking`) and every stroke after it lands whichever way the hull is going. A key HELD is one stroke however long it is held and a key TAPPED is a stroke a tap, and how many taps a trick costs is `riderAuthority / I` and no per-craft knob. The whip alone has a SIDE: the bars crossing the centre end the throw that was running. The bot's air caps are its OWN numbers (`airLeanCap`, `airBarsCap` in `sim/bot.ts`) and not the gates — a levelling PD handed the gates' authority over-rotates before any stroke is involved — and the steer cap is read off `CraftState.launchVy` rather than off `airTime`, because the bot decides a step before the engine reads the gate and one uncapped step is a whole throw.
- **HOW MUCH HELP THE RIDER IS GIVEN** — `GameState.assist` and `GameState.rampAssist`, the arcade's two dials (`engine/game/assist.ts`, `TUNING.assist.air` / `.ramp`): the landing caught at the end of a flight, and the slide taken out of a run up a ramp's deck. Each is 0..1, read and never written during a run, drawing no randomness — so a run replays identically at any setting and `createGame({ assist: 0 })` is the bare physics everywhere. A difficulty setting moves these two numbers and nothing else; no surface keeps a second opinion about how much help is on.

- **WHAT A TRICK IS WORTH** — `engine/game/tricks.ts`, and `TUNING.tricks` is every number behind it. Air time pays by the SECOND at a rate rising with the flight (`airPointsPerSecond`, logarithmic in how long the hull has been up, so a flight's purse grows faster than the flight does) AND THE JUMP'S LENGTH PAYS BY THE METRE on the same curve drawn in distance (`lengthPointsPerMetre`, off `CraftState.airLength` — the plan distance from where the hull left, carried as the furthest the flight reached). The two are TIED AT ONE SPEED and there is only one number for it: `tricks.lengthKnee` (17.5 m, the metres an ordinary ramp carries a rider in the air's own 1 s knee — measured over the campaign's four tricks shores), off which the rate is `airRate · airKnee / lengthKnee`, stated in `tricks.ts` and never beside `airRate` where it would be a second copy of the tie. A flight at that speed earns the same by the metre as by the second, so an ordinary jump is worth twice its air alone and the two only come apart where they should — a pop straight off a crest is all seconds, a flat drive off a lip is all metres. The length buys no element and no rung: the flight is already named by the air's own, and a second rung would sell one jump twice. A revolution pays base AND a step of multiplier, both rising with the revolution's index, so a double is ×4 rather than ×3; the two AXES are counted apart (`rotation`/`spins` nose-over-tail off the body pitch rate, `roll`/`rolls` about the hull's length off the body roll rate — both summed while aloft, the same readings `flight_test` measures a backflip with), so a flip with a roll in it is two FIRST revolutions; the FLIGHT ITSELF is an element worth one more step, credited only beside a trick and only once per combo, adding no base because the seconds are already paid; and nothing is banked until the combo closes with the rider still on the craft. Nothing else anywhere prices a moment: the HUD, the news column and the sim's `score` column all read `GameState.tricks` or the `trick` / `combo` / `bail` events. **No WORD for any of it is in the engine** — `TrickKind` names the thing and `TrickPart` carries the revolutions and which flight of the combo won it; the vocabulary is `pwa/src/game/strings.ts`'s (§39.1), including its one compound (the engine's `corkscrew` element is named in place of the flip and the roll it was made of).

  THREE OF THE ELEMENTS ARE NOT TURNED IN THE AIR. The CORKSCREW is both axes coming round in ONE flight — won as the second of them closes, once per flight, and priced for the combination rather than for the turns, which were already paid at their own index. The CREST RIDE is the top of a wave HELD: paid by the second on the air's own curve at `waveRate` (`wavePointsPerSecond`), an element in its own right past `waveElement`, and standing ALONE where the air does not, because every ramp hands a rider a flight and nothing hands him a crest. The LAYDOWN is the hull heeled past `laydownAngle` and brought back level, judged off the PEAK of the heel rather than a latch so a capsize saved cannot claim one. What counts as being on a crest is `wave-ride.ts`'s and never restated (below).

- **WHAT WAVE A RIDER IS ON** — `waveUnder(sea, level, x, z, t, out)` in `engine/game/wave-ride.ts`: the wave passing his own plan point, read by sweeping the surface across one peak period of whichever band is carrying the sea there (`seaSummary`) — the ZERO-CROSSING measure `make surf` counts waves with, because the sea here is four bands of a hundred-odd Gerstner terms and no one of them is "the wave". What comes back is the crest-to-trough and where in it the water under him stands. `ridingCrest` is the three-part rule laid over it — a wave with `waveHeight` in it, the hull at `waveCrest` of the way up (falling back only to `waveHold` once the ride is running), and `underWay`: the rider making `riding` m/s. There is no term for "along the wave" and none is needed — holding the top IS running along it, and a hull driven ACROSS the sea cannot hold it for a fifth as long. Pure in (place, clock) like `surfaceAt` and `faunaPose`, so nothing is stored and a run replays crest for crest.

- **WHAT A RUN IS PLAYING BY** — `GameState.rules` (`RunRules`, `engine/game/defs/modes.ts`): whether the course counts, whether the tricks do, how many rivals there are, how long the lights hold and whether a buzzer ends it. A MODE is a named bundle of them (`MODE_RULES`) and nothing below the app branches on a mode's name; `createGame` without a mode deals `OPEN_RULES` — every system on, no lights, nobody else on the water — which is what the sim, the labs and every existing test ride, so no digest moved when the modes landed. `run.ts` reads the rules; `craft.ts` reads one (`tricks`, to leave the strokes unread). A RIVAL is a whole `GameState` over the same world (`rivals.ts`) — the level, the sea, the wind, the rules and the stream are the player's own objects — so the field is stepped by the very function the player is and ridden by the very bot the sim rides; what tells one from the next is `Rival.pace`, dealt off the stream once at the grid. `racePlace` is the one reading of where the player stands in it.
- **WHICH GENERATOR BUILT A SHORE** — `engine/mapgen/versions.ts`. `CURRENT_GENERATOR_VERSION` is the rules in this tree and is what everything but a campaign level gets; a campaign level names its own (`CampaignLevel.version`, written twelve times on purpose, one decision each) and carries the DIGEST of the shore that came out (`levelDigest`), which `tests/generator_version_test.ts` rebuilds and compares. A change that moves what a seed builds owes a version row with the old behaviour kept as a trait — never a regenerated digest, which is the silent re-roll the scheme exists to prevent. Full contract: `docs/level-generator.md`.
- **WHICH SHORE A MEASURED RUN IS ON** — `pinnedFor(settings)` in `pwa/src/game/new-game.ts`, over `fitsMode` / `levelForMode` in `campaign.ts`. A RACE, a TRICKS run and a TIME TRIAL are ridden on one of the campaign's twelve pinned levels rather than on a seed, so that two figures down one of them are two figures down the same water; FREE is the one mode that picks a seed and sets the day itself, and a link that names a `seed` takes the pinned shore off for the same reason (`url-params.ts`). A level rides only in the discipline it was CURATED in — R35's trick field is laid at build time, so the same seed asked for ramps is not always the same shore — and what `gameFor`, `recordKeyFor` and the level card offer all read this one answer. What the campaign then adds on top is the points and the locks, and nothing else.
- **WHAT A CAMPAIGN RUN IS** — `campaignGame` in `pwa/src/game/campaign.ts`: the pinned shore under the pinned day, in the level's own mode, with the race's grid on it and `rules.contact` off — the one place a run is stood up from a table rather than from the start card, and the one place the field is asked not to touch. What the finish is worth is `recordRun` beside it: the field filed in `fieldOrder`'s order, the podium paid, the better afternoon kept.
- **The ONE clock** — `state.t` advances by `TUNING.dt` per step and is the only time the engine knows; the sea is a function of it. Nothing in `engine/` reads a wall clock (`analyzeLevel`'s report timer is the recorded exception, dev-time only).

## Test conventions

- Tests live in the root `tests/` directory, one file per topic, named `<topic>_test.ts` (OSS_GAME_SPEC §20.2).
- Runner: vitest via `make test`; config in `vitest.config.ts` (alias `@engine` → `engine/index.ts`, `testTimeout` 120 s because a case builds whole levels). No DOM, no browser — engine tests, plus the DOM-free app modules (`input-model.ts`, `scenarios.ts`, `identity.ts`, and the audio's vocabulary, bank, route and beds under a recording synth).
- **Physics tests stage the craft on the SYNTHETIC level** in `tests/support/synthetic.ts` — a flat bed, a straight shore, a row of gates, one ramp, two skerries, nothing the generator built — and stand it at a moment with `placeRun` (`engine/game/place.ts`), then script inputs step by step. That is the §23.8 sequel test kept honest: the rule suite passes with the generator deleted. The `test-scenario` skill owns staging an exact situation.
- **A file that asserts a dozen rules over the same spread of seeds takes its levels from `tests/support/levels.ts`** (`LEVEL_SEEDS`, `levelFor`, `analysisFor`) rather than generating them per `it`. Generating a level is the most expensive thing the engine does (a coast, a course, the rocks, two baked grids, an analysis, and again for every rejected sub-seed) and it is deterministic, so the second build can only return the first one's answer. What comes back is SHARED and read-only; a test that breaks a level on purpose copies it first, and a determinism check that needs two independent builds calls the engine directly.
- **Sharding splits at FILE granularity, so the slowest single file is the floor under `make test` on CI.** Four shards is where a suite this size flattens. Keep a file under a minute: share the corpus, and split a file whose subject is really two (`mapgen_test` / `mapgen_population_test`).
- **The governance tests are tests too**, and they run in the same suite: `imports_test` (the dependency direction, §23.7), `file_size_test` (§20.5), `symlinks_test` (§7.1, §21.2), `identity_test` (§35.6), `changeset_test` (§8.5), `skills_test` (§21), `docs_rules_test` (the rule book's mirror), `determinism_test` (§25), `tauri_test` (§23.3 — the desktop shell's restatement of the identity). A structural rule this file states and no test holds is a rule that will not survive a deadline.
- **Running them:** `make test` is the whole suite (`SHARD=i/N` runs one slice); `npx vitest run tests/<topic>_test.ts` runs one file, which is what you run locally.
- No extra test dependencies; everything runs on plain Node.

## Parity and cross-cutting rules

Places where one idea is deliberately written in two files that cannot import each other. Each is a live trap: change one, change both.

- `pwa/src/identity.ts` is the identity source of truth; `pwa/public/icons/icon.svg`, `scripts/generate-icons.mjs` and `pwa/src/game/app-mark.ts` encode the same mark geometry (the wave's two arcs) and the same palette hexes. None can import either of the others, so change one and change all three, then `make icons`. `tests/identity_test.ts` holds the generator's palette to `PALETTE`, and `tests/app_mark_test.ts` holds the SVG's two paths and stroke width to `app-mark.ts`.
- `pwa/index.html` restates the name and `PALETTE.sea` (a static head cannot import); `pwa/public/CNAME` restates the domain. **THE SITE IS DELIBERATELY NOT INDEXED** — the head carries `noindex` and no description, canonical, Open Graph, Twitter card or JSON-LD, the body prerenders no copy, `robots.txt` is `Disallow: /`, and `sitemap.xml`, `llms.txt` and `og.png` are not shipped. `tests/identity_test.ts` holds all of it from both sides, so adding a discovery signal back fails the suite; `docs/configuration.md` § *Discoverability* is the description and `docs/spec-conformance.md` carries it as a deliberate deviation from §11.3.
- The service worker contract (the cache id, the emitted files) is shared between `pwa/pwa-plugin.ts` and `pwa/src/app-pwa.ts` (`cacheIdForBase`) — keep them agreeing.
- **The rule book has a mirror, and THREE CHAPTERS.** `engine/mapgen/rules.ts` (R1–R28, a coast sprint), `engine/mapgen/rules-circuit.ts` (R29–R31, the ocean circuit that replaces R1, R10, R25 and R26 on a level drawn as one) and `engine/mapgen/pace.ts` (R32–R37: what any of them becomes at a speed class, and the dials a run sets for itself — the ramp's width, the turn a GATE may ask for, the tricks field, the sea standing off the coast, the winter's ice; they live there because the first chapter is AT the §20.5 cap, which is where a new rule's prose goes when it cannot fit beside its own table) each state their rules once in the file header; `docs/level-generator.md` carries the same prose VERBATIM for all three. R26 and R27's TABLE is a fourth file, `engine/mapgen/rules-river.ts`, folded back in under the same names (`R.river`, `R.flow`) — the `defs/sea.ts` pattern, not a chapter: no rule is STATED there, so nothing new has to be mirrored. `tests/docs_rules_test.ts` reads the ids off all three files, so a new rule fails the test until its mirror lands. The split is the §20.5 cap, not two rule books: `LEVEL_RULES.circuit` is the second chapter's table and everything reads the numbers through the one `LEVEL_RULES` — except a RUN'S OWN DIALS (`RAMP_DIAL`, `SWELL_DIAL`), which are the third chapter's and are stated beside the rule that owns them.
- **A GHOST AND A REPLAY ARE ONE TAPE.** `ControlTape` in `pwa/src/game/ghost.ts` is the six RLE'd, base64'd control streams and the step count, and `createControlRecorder` / `readControls` are the only encoder and decoder of them. A ghost is that tape plus the figure it beat (`GhostRun`, kept in `localStorage`); a replay is the same tape plus how to rebuild the afternoon (`replay.ts`, kept only as long as the tab is). A second copy of the encoding would be a ghost and a replay that drift apart the day an axis is added, so `replay.ts` imports the codec rather than restating it — and `snapInput` above both is why either is exact rather than merely close.
- **The fauna is named twice.** `engine/game/defs/fauna.ts` (with the warm coast's rows next door in `defs/fauna-warm.ts` and the polar coast's in `defs/fauna-arctic.ts`, concatenated into the one `FAUNA`) carries what an animal IS (its length, its speed, how rare it is) and `pwa/src/game/fauna-styles.ts`'s `STYLES` carries what it LOOKS like (its paint, its fins, its markings) — the craft's `defs/craft.ts` / `craft-styles.ts` split, and the same trap: a species added to one and not the other does not compile, but a species whose proportions disagree between them draws wrong and nothing says so.
- **A coast is named SEVEN times.** `engine/mapgen/biomes.ts` carries what a coast IS (its stone, its relief and how steeply it climbs, its beach, its river's shape and the bars in its mouth, its water's density and temperature, its latitude, its sea's size, its skies, its sea life); `pwa/src/game/water-optics.ts` what its WATER LOOKS like (its tones, its ramp, its window, its clarity, its bottom); `shore-paint.ts` what its SHORE is painted; `sky-looks.ts` what its SKIES and SEASONS do to the air; `colour-grade.ts` what its whole finished PICTURE is graded; and every row of `flora-defs.ts` and `bird-defs.ts` names the coasts it belongs to — the `defs/fauna.ts` / `STYLES` split, for the same reason: nothing in the engine has an opinion about colour. None can import another, so a coast added to `BIOMES` and missing from any of them throws on its first level or grows nothing; `tests/biome_test.ts` holds all seven lists to `BIOME_IDS`, and holds the tree to naming no place.
- **The audition page links by CONCATENATION.** `RUNTIME` in `scripts/audition.mjs` lists the audio modules the page inlines, in dependency order, and only `export function` / `export const` names cross a module's scope; a bed's spec table that is not `export const`, or a module a bed calls that is not listed before it, is a page that builds clean and throws on the first button. `ride-bed.ts` is deliberately NOT on the list — it reads a `GameState` — so a bed's whole voice must stay in its `*-voice.ts` target function.
- **The staged moments are named THREE times.** `pwa/src/game/scenarios.ts` is the app's list (`?scene=`); `scripts/lib/ride-scenarios.mjs` is the ride lab's, which stages its own approach rather than the app's photograph; and `SCENES` in `scripts/screenshot.mjs` restates the app's names so `--all` knows what to ask for. A scenario added to the first and not the second is a lab that cannot draw what the app can stand in; added to the first and not the third it is simply never photographed by `--all`, silently, which is how `tuck` went unshot. Change one, change all three — or fold the two lists onto the app's through `aliasEngine`.
- **The sky is authored in linear light and drawn in three.js.** `pwa/src/game/sky.ts` is deliberately three-free so the tests can read the whole colour model, and every mix in it goes through `pwa/src/lib/colour.ts`, which converts sRGB→linear→sRGB exactly the way `THREE.Color.lerp` does with colour management on. A mix added in the sky against a different curve drifts from every material the same preset lights. **A hand-written `ShaderMaterial` has to close that loop itself**: `THREE.Color.set(hex)` converts sRGB→linear on the way in, so a shader that writes its result straight to `gl_FragColor` skips the conversion back and hands out a picture about half as bright as the one authored. Every one of ours ends with `#include <colorspace_fragment>`; the fault does not read as "too dark" but as a far shore glowing brighter than the sky behind it.
- **The grade is one arithmetic in two languages.** `gradeTone` in `pwa/src/game/colour-grade.ts` is the model on plain numbers and `grade-pass.ts`'s fragment shader is the same five steps in GLSL, in the same order and with the same two clamps. Neither can import the other — one is three-free on purpose, the other is nothing but three — so `tests/colour_grade_test.ts` holds the shader to the model by reading it as TEXT: every dial has a uniform, every uniform is read, the pivot is the model's, the steps are in order and both clamps are there. Only the shader ever runs in the game; the model is how a grade is ARGUED about, and reasoning about the shader instead is how a cast half a stop dark ships.
- **The ramp is one line in two engines.** `rampSurface` (`mapgen/course.ts`) and `rampDeckY` (`game/collision.ts`) compute the same deck height from the same hinge; `tests/collision_test.ts` rides one and `tests/mapgen_test.ts` places rings off the other.
- **THE DESKTOP SHELL RESTATES THE IDENTITY IN RUST.** `pwa/src/shell-host.ts` states the `__SH_SHELL__` global, both fullscreen events and the menu's word list; `tauri/shell/src/config.rs` and `menu.rs` spell every one of them again, and `pwa/src/identity.ts`'s name, description and `PALETTE.sea` are restated in `tauri/src-tauri/tauri.conf.json` and `config.rs`. Neither side can import the other, so `tests/tauri_test.ts` holds all of it together — reading the Rust as TEXT, so the root suite needs no toolchain. A word added to one side alone is a menu row that silently does nothing. **The store app restates the same global in JavaScript** — `NATIVE_FLAG` in `native/src/injected.ts`, injected before the page's own scripts — and one of the menu's words besides: `SHOT_COMMAND` in the same file presses `sh-shell-command`'s `shot` when the phone takes a screenshot of its own. `tests/shell_test.ts` holds that half, by RUNNING the script and feeding what it dispatches to the page's own listener.
- **A PULSE IS NAMED THREE TIMES.** The event (`SHELL_RUMBLE` in `pwa/src/shell-host.ts`), the listener that posts it (`RUMBLE_BRIDGE` in `native/src/injected.ts`) and the parser that reads the message (`parseRumble` in `native/src/rumble.ts`) are three files that cannot import each other, and a rename in one of them is a phone that silently stops buzzing. `tests/rumble_test.ts` holds all three. The WEBSITE owns the feature all the same: what is felt and how big it is is `pwa/src/game/rumble.ts`, the one motor and the player's switch are `pwa/src/game/haptics.ts`, and the shell only plays what it is handed.
- The deployed site IS the product (§11.2-as-webapp): there is no separate `website/` tree, and no marketing copy either — the page says nothing about the game outside the running app. Keep what is left (the title, the theme colour, the manifest, the privacy and support pages) in sync with `identity.ts`, and treat a stale deployed site after identity/feature changes as a bug (`update-website` owns the sweep).
- The engine's determinism is a contract three suites hold from three sides — `determinism_test` (a run replays), `simulation_test` (the digest), `imports_test` (no clock, no `Math.random`, nothing external) — and the wind consumes its randomness whether or not anything feels it, so adding a draw anywhere in `step` changes every digest: say so in the PR.

## Skills

Skills live in `.agents/skills/` (`.claude/skills` and `.gemini/skills` symlink there) — each a `SKILL.md` playbook with an empty `.lessons/` directory `skill-reflection` fills. Load the one that owns the task's SUBJECT, plus the workflow ones its steps name. This file is the router; the procedures live in the skills. `tests/skills_test.ts` holds this list to the directory.

**Session workflow** (every task):

- **`start-work`** — the preflight: clean tree, sync with `origin/main`, the deliver-by-default contract.
- **`write-code`** — how code is written here: comments and the comment-pruning pass, the edit loop, the 1000-line cap, test conventions, the generic pools and aliases. Load beside the subject skill on any code change.
- **`skill-reflection`** — read each loaded skill's lessons at the start (`node scripts/skill-lessons.mjs <skill>`), record/prune/promote at the end.
- **`changelog`** → **`commit`** — the fragment-or-label call, then gates, push, PR. **`conflict`** whenever a branch moves onto another.

**Craft** (the subject owners):

- **`game-feel`** — how the game FEELS: the hull meeting a wave, the reference (the 90s jetski racers with a modern look), the camera, the cross-system levers. Load it whenever the acceptance test is "does it feel right".
- **`water-feel`** — the sea: the Gerstner sum, the JONSWAP / Pierson–Moskowitz spectrum, dispersion, shoaling, breaking, the fetch law and the exposure that decides whether a point is dealt the ocean's sea or a river's chop (R28), the river's current (R27), the gusts and the shelter field; `make waves`.
- **`craft-physics`** — the hull's answer to the water: the probes and the draft, the drags, Savitsky's planing lift, the slam and the dive, the waterjet and the nozzle, the lean, flight; `make ride`.
- **`craft-tuning`** — what separates the skiff, the marlin, the otter and the dart; the catalog and its derived expectations; the roster read off `make sim`.
- **`craft-design`** — how a craft LOOKS: the parametric builder, the styles, the `SCENE=rest` contact sheet.
- **`rider`** — the man on the saddle: the pose from the cockpit and the engine's readings, the body on springs, the figure; judged from behind on the sheet and at chase range.
- **`collision`** — the hull meeting what is not water: solids, grounding, ramps, gates and misses, the bounds; what each event means.
- **`engine-system`** — adding or changing a gameplay system, engine-first.
- **`mapgen-improvement`** — the shore generator (rules / search / geometry, the R-rules), the analyze → fix → `make level` loop.
- **`nature`** — the shore's materials as biome-as-data, what `terrain.ts` paints, the rocks, the sea life under the water (R20: the catalog and its rarity, the placer, the swim model, the look), the cover above the waterline (the roster, its habitats, `make flora`), and the birds over both (the roster, where a flock lives, how it flies, what crosses in which season, `make birds`).
- **`atmosphere`** — the sky and the air under it: the sun's place by hour, season and latitude, the ladder of looks, what each weather puts over it, the clouds, the night (the stars, the moon's key, the lamps), the haze, the rain; `make sky`.
- **`water-look`** — the sea as DRAWN: the grid of rings and the far grid, how the surface is lit (the sky and the shore it mirrors, the glint, the ripples, the foam), a coast's water optics, how far the eye gets into it; judged at two skies, zoomed.
- **`visual-effects`** — what the craft throws off and what the rider feels: the spray, the wake as a map the water shader draws, the footprints, the vibration table; event → effect, presentation only.
- **`platform-shells`** — the desktop app (`tauri/`: two crates, decisions and effects) and the store app (`native/`: the WebView, the local server, the haptics bridge), the `__SH_SHELL__` seam and the names stated twice.
- **`lab-tooling`** — how a lab or a script is built here: the `scripts/lib/` shelf, pure-Node versus browser-driven, the harness-page pattern, the URL contract, and registering a tool. Load it BEFORE writing a one-off script.
- **`hud-and-menus`** — the HUD's readouts, the handlebar and the throttle lever, the keys — what is drawn over a RUN.
- **`menu-system`** — the shell around a run: the attract card, the front door, the start card and the craft card after it, options, the developer page behind the seven-second hold, the loading card, the pause card that holds a run mid-ride, and the settings they read and write.
- **`ui-review`** — the fit-and-finish sweep at the reference viewports (1280×720, 390×844).
- **`playtest`** — staged moments photographed in the built app: `make screenshots SCENE=`.
- **`test-scenario`** — exact situations: the synthetic level, `placeRun`, scripted inputs, `scenarios.ts` read three ways.
- **`debug-game`** — deterministic repros, classifying by layer, the failing test first.
- **`simulate-run`** — `make sim`: the `RunReport` table, its columns, which movements are regressions.
- **`bot-improvement`** — the gate-aiming bot in `engine/sim/bot.ts`, kept minimal, measured with `make sim`.
- **`level-rating`** — whether a generated level is any GOOD and how HARD: `engine/rating/`'s eight axes and the index, the scales calibrated off a sweep (`make rate ARGS=--stats`), the ladder scorer, and `make difficulty`'s schematic.
- **`campaign`** — the twelve pinned levels and the ladder they make (`campaign-levels.ts`, `campaign.ts`, `menu-campaign.tsx`): curating a rung with `make rate CAMPAIGN=1` and the sim, the generator-version contract a pinned shore stands on, the points and the locks.
- **`sound-effects`** — every sound, synthesized: the vocabulary and the instrument, the bank and the route, the beds (the engine and the pump, the hull, the wind, the sea) and the listener; `make audition`, and `--meter` for a session that cannot listen.

**Maintenance** (each with a `.last-updated` baseline):

- **`maintenance`** — the umbrella: dispatches every `update-*` skill in registry order after big merges or on a cadence.
- **`update-docs`** / **`update-readme`** / **`update-website`** / **`update-prompts`** — re-sync `docs/*.md`, README.md, the identity shell, and `prompts/` (dormant) against their sources of truth.
- **`sync-game-spec`** — walk OSS_GAME_SPEC.md chapter by chapter against the repo, offline, and re-date `docs/spec-conformance.md`; the closing step of a full sweep.

Run the specific skill when you know what drifted; run `maintenance` when you don't.

**Reserved, not written** (see `.agents/skills/README.md`): soundtrack, wipeout, damage, tricks, craft-creation, built-shore, fauna, replay (the app's recording and its broadcast camera are built and routed above; the skill waits for the engine-side tape), debug-tools, store-listing, store-shots. A lesson about one of those subjects waits, scoped, in the nearest existing skill until its subject is built.
